## 1. NDPA 2023 + GAID 2025: lawful bases, consent, sensitive data, rights, DPO, DPIA, breach notification, CAR filing, DPCOs

### Takeaway
The NDPA 2023 and the GAID are **[IN FORCE]**. The GAID was issued on 20 March 2025, took effect on 19 September 2025 and repealed the NDPR 2019. HomeAI needs a documented lawful basis for each processing purpose. It needs explicit consent for automated decisions with legal effect, for cross-border transfers when consent is the transfer basis, and for direct marketing. It must notify a breach to the NDPC within 72 hours. Because it will process far more than 200 data subjects, it will almost certainly be a "Data Controller of Major Importance" (DCPMI). That means registering with the NDPC, appointing a DPO and, at EHL or UHL tier, filing an annual Compliance Audit Return (CAR) through a licensed DPCO by 31 March. Financial data and NIN/BVN are **not** listed as "sensitive personal data" in the NDPA definition. Biometric data used for identification, such as a selfie or liveness check, **is** sensitive.

### Cited Findings
**Status and scope**
- The GAID was issued on 20 March 2025 and became operational on 19 September 2025 — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/); [Mondaq – Unlocking GAID 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- The GAID repeals the NDPR 2019 and its 2020 Implementation Framework. Actions taken under the NDPR remain valid — [Afriwise / Olaniwun Ajayi](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)
- Where another law or guideline is inconsistent with the NDPA on personal data processing, the NDPA prevails — [Mondaq GAID highlights (via search)](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1624030/highlights-of-the-nigeria-data-protection-act-general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)
- Physical presence in Nigeria is not required. A foreign controller or processor that targets Nigerian data subjects can fall in the DCPMI category — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- In August 2025 the NDPC sent notices giving organisations 21 days, until 15 September 2025, to prove compliance — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)

**Lawful bases and consent**
- The NDPA lawful bases are consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest or official authority, and legitimate interests. Legitimate interests must not override data subjects' rights and must match their reasonable expectations — [DLA Piper Data Protection Laws – Nigeria](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- Before collecting data, the controller must disclose its identity, DPO contact details, purposes, legal basis, recipients, transfer intentions, retention periods and data subject rights — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- GAID Art. 17–18: consent should be prioritised. Consent is **required** for direct marketing, sensitive data, incompatible further processing, children's data, cross-border transfers, and automated decisions with legal effect. Controllers must keep consent records and make withdrawal easy, and refusing consent must not harm the data subject — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- The GAID includes a Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA) framework — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)
- GAID Art. 19 on cookies: consent is needed for all non-necessary cookies. The banner must be prominent without scrolling and offer explicit accept and reject options. Similar tracking tools follow the same rules — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- A clear privacy notice and a clear cookie notice must be published (GAID Art. 7) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)

**Sensitive data**
- Under the NDPA, "sensitive personal data" means genetic and biometric data used for unique identification, race or ethnic origin, religious or similar beliefs, health status, sex life, political opinions or affiliations, trade union membership, and other categories the Commission prescribes — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- Some Nigerian commentary informally treats "financial details" as sensitive, but this is not in the statutory definition — [Banwo & Ighodalo (via search)](https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/?p=25941)
- The GAID sets up a "vulnerable data subjects" regime covering, among others, people in financial difficulty, people lacking education or digital literacy, and elderly people — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)

**Data subject rights**
- Data subjects have rights of access, rectification, withdrawal of consent and complaint. They can object to direct marketing at any time. The GAID adds the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions and the right to be forgotten in emerging-technology contexts — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- Systems must support access, correction and portability (GAID Art. 7). Erasure has exceptions for legal claims, public interest and similar grounds (Art. 38) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- GAID Art. 40 creates the SNAG (Standard Notice to Address Grievance). A data subject, a representative or a civil society organisation may issue one, and the controller must respond. A SNAG is not a precondition to complaining to the NDPC or going to court — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- Retention: where no specific retention period applies, personal data must be deleted within **six months** after its purpose is fulfilled. It may be kept longer, securely, for legal defence or due diligence — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)

**DPO**
- A DCPMI must designate a DPO, who may be an employee or engaged under a service contract — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- GAID Arts. 11–14 on the DPO:
  - The DPO's contact details must be sent to the NDPC.
  - The DPO reports directly to management and must have no conflict of interest.
  - The DPO prepares a **semi-annual** data protection report.
  - The NDPC keeps a database of certified DPOs and runs annual credential assessments, including CPD.
  - Sources: [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)
- Commercial estimates for DPO training and certification are ₦200,000–₦1,500,000 per person. These are not official fees — [Global Law Experts](https://globaladvisoryexperts.com/ndpc-data-controller-registration/)

**DPIA**
- A DPIA is required where processing may pose substantial risk. If the DPIA shows high risk, the controller must consult the NDPC before processing — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- GAID DPIA triggers include:
  - profiling or scoring
  - automated decisions with significant effects
  - systematic monitoring
  - sensitive data
  - vulnerable data subjects
  - innovative technology
  - financial services via digital devices
  - e-commerce
  - cross-border transfers
  - Sources: [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- GAID Schedule 4 provides a 10-section DPIA template — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)

**Breach notification**
- Notify the NDPC within **72 hours** of becoming aware of a breach likely to risk individuals' rights. Notify data subjects **immediately** if the breach is likely to result in high risk; a public communication is allowed where direct notice is disproportionate. A processor must notify the controller on becoming aware of a breach — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG); 72-hour rule also in [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)

**Compliance Audit Returns (CAR) and DPCOs**
- UHL and EHL entities register once and then file an annual CAR. OHL entities renew registration annually and do **not** file a CAR (GAID Arts. 8–10) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)
- The CAR is due **31 March** each year for the previous year. A new entity files within **15 months** of establishment. Late filing carries a **50% surcharge** on the filing fee — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- The NDPC extended the deadline for 2025 CARs from 31 March 2026 to **30 May 2026** — [Mondaq regulatory update, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1770448/regulatory-update-ndpc-extends-data-audit-filing-deadline). One secondary site instead gives 15 March 2026 as the original statutory date — [Global Law Experts (via search)](https://globallawexperts.com/nigeria-data-protection-compliance-2026/)
- **GAID CAR filing fees** (the old NDPR fees were ₦10,000 for under 2,000 data subjects and ₦20,000 for 2,000 or more) — [Mondaq regulatory update](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1770448/regulatory-update-ndpc-extends-data-audit-filing-deadline); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)

  | Tier | Data subjects | CAR filing fee |
  |---|---|---|
  | UHL Tier A | 50,000 and above | ₦1,000,000 |
  | UHL Tier B | 25,000–49,999 | ₦750,000 |
  | UHL Tier C | below 25,000 | ₦500,000 |
  | EHL Tier A | 10,000 and above | ₦250,000 |
  | EHL Tier B | 2,500–9,999 | ₦200,000 |
  | EHL Tier C | below 2,500 | ₦100,000 |

  **Conflict:** Afriwise gives EHL Tier B as 5,000–9,999 rather than 2,500–9,999, which leaves a 2,500–4,999 gap.
- UHL and EHL filings must go through a licensed DPCO (GAID Art. 10) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/). **Conflict:** Mondaq's April 2026 update calls using a DPCO "recommended", not mandatory — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1770448/regulatory-update-ndpc-extends-data-audit-filing-deadline)
- A DPCO-led annual audit commonly costs ₦1,000,000–₦5,000,000+ (commercial estimate). The DPCO licence fee is industry-reported at about ₦2,000,000 (unverified) — [Global Law Experts](https://globaladvisoryexperts.com/ndpc-data-controller-registration/)
- Under the GAID, emerging technologies such as AI require a DPIA before deployment, sandbox testing and continuous monitoring, with documentation filed with the NDPC as part of the CAR — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)

**Penalties and enforcement**
- A DCPMI faces fines up to the greater of ₦10,000,000 or 2% of the previous year's annual gross revenue. Other controllers face up to the greater of ₦2,000,000 or 2%. The NDPC can also order compensation, disgorgement of profits and suspension of processing. Failing to comply with an NDPC order is a criminal offence carrying up to 1 year's imprisonment — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- The NDPC fined Multichoice Nigeria ₦766.2 million — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- Reported enforcement figures, all single-outlet and unverified:
  - more than 38,000 organisations registered as DCPMIs
  - 246 investigations concluded
  - about ₦7.2bn collected from registrations, compliance fees and fines
  - Sources: [Techmoonshot, Feb 2026](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/02/10/nigeria-just-collected-%E2%82%A67-2-billion-in-data-privacy-penalties-and-its-just-getting-started/); [The Economic Times NG, Jan 2026](https://theeconomictimes.com.ng/2026/01/21/businesses-face-up-to-n10m-fine-as-ndpc-tightens-data-protection-audit-enforcement/)
- GAID Art. 32 (abetment): on NDPC notice, a controller must immediately restrict an offending party, for example a rogue processor or user. Failing to do so is treated as a direct violation — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)

### Inferences
- HomeAI will likely be at least EHL, and possibly UHL once it passes 5,000 data subjects in 6 months. Tenants, landlords and agents all count as data subjects. Plan for one-time registration, an annual CAR through a DPCO by 31 March, and a CAR fee of ₦100,000–₦1,000,000 depending on volume. The first CAR falls due within 15 months of starting.
- Suggested lawful bases:
  - **Contract** for core account and listing functions
  - **Legal obligation or legitimate interest** (with an LIA) for KYC and fraud checks
  - **Explicit consent** for marketing, non-essential cookies, AI-scored applications, biometric liveness checks and, where relied on, foreign transfers
- Treat income, employment and financial data as high-risk even though they are not statutorily "sensitive". Rental applicants in financial difficulty fall within the GAID's "vulnerable data subjects" regime, and the DPIA triggers cover financial services and profiling.
- A DPIA is effectively mandatory for HomeAI. Its AI scoring, cross-border transfers, e-commerce-style activity and vulnerable users each trigger one under the GAID list.
- Practical steps:
  1. Run a data map and RoPA.
  2. Publish a privacy notice and a cookie banner with an equal "Reject" option.
  3. Build a consent log.
  4. Build a DSAR and SNAG workflow.
  5. Apply the six-month post-purpose deletion default to rejected applications.
  6. Prepare a breach runbook with a 72-hour NDPC clock and processor-to-controller notice clauses.
  7. Appoint a DPO, internal or outsourced, and diarise the semi-annual DPO report.

### Gaps
- The official GAID PDF (articles and schedules) was not retrieved directly. Article numbers come from law-firm summaries.
- No statutory deadline for answering data subject requests was found in this session.
- No source in this session stated an explicit "consent must be in writing" rule for sensitive data under the GAID.
- Whether a DPCO is legally mandatory for CAR filing is disputed between sources.

## 2. Registration as a "Data Controller/Processor of Major Importance": criteria, tiers, fees, deadlines

### Takeaway
DCPMI status under the 2024 Guidance Notice, carried into GAID Art. 8, is mainly volume-based:
- **OHL**: more than 200 data subjects in 6 months
- **EHL**: more than 1,000
- **UHL**: more than 5,000

Certain sectors are designated regardless of volume. Registration fees reported from the February 2024 Guidance Notice are OHL ₦10,000 (renewed annually), EHL ₦100,000 and UHL ₦250,000 (both one-time). Fee figures in commentary are inconsistent, and an updated notice followed a court ruling, so the live portal must be checked. **[OLDER INFO – VERIFY]**

### Cited Findings
- Tier thresholds:
  - OHL: 200–1,000 data subjects in 6 months; fee ₦10,000, renewed annually; no CAR
  - EHL: 1,000–5,000; fee ₦100,000, one-time; annual CAR by 31 March through a DPCO
  - UHL: more than 5,000; fee ₦250,000, one-time; annual CAR through a DPCO
  - MDAs, telecoms, banks and fintechs are designated regardless of volume
  - Source: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- The same 2024 fees (UHL ₦250,000, EHL ₦100,000, OHL ₦10,000) appear in summaries of the Guidance Notice effective 14 February 2024 — [Afriwise](https://insights.afriwise.com/blog/guidance-notice-issued-by-the-nigerian-data-protection-commission); [Templars client alert](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/02/CLIENT-ALERT-NDPC-ISSUES-GUIDANCE-NOTICE.pdf)
- Example sectors: UHL covers banks, telecoms and multinationals; EHL covers MDAs and microfinance banks; OHL covers schools and health centres. Thresholds are over 5,000, over 1,000 and over 200 data subjects in 6 months — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- **Conflict:** the same Mondaq piece gives *registration* fees of UHL ₦500,000–₦1,000,000 and EHL ₦100,000–₦250,000. These match the CAR fee bands and are probably a conflation — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- **Conflict:** another guide describes presumptive DCPMI thresholds of about 2,000 data subjects for sensitive data and 10,000+ for ordinary data over 12 months. It also lists qualitative criteria such as credit scoring and profiling. This does not match the 200/1,000/5,000 framework — [Global Law Experts](https://globaladvisoryexperts.com/ndpc-data-controller-registration/)
- An updated Guidance Notice (NDPC/HQ/GN/VOL.03/B/24) changed registration requirements after a Federal High Court declared some provisions of the earlier notice void — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Compliance-Analysis_Considerations-on-the-Updated-Registration-of-DCPMI-Aluko_Oyebode.pdf)
- Registration deadline: within 6 months of becoming a DCPMI. Registration changes must be notified within 60 days (GAID Art. 9(4)). Outstanding fees remain payable after deregistration (Art. 9(5)–(6)). The NDPC publishes the register annually (Art. 9(7)) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions); [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- Portal process:
  1. Create an organisation account on the NDPC portal.
  2. Choose controller, processor or both.
  3. Declare data types, data-subject volume, transfers and the DPO.
  4. Upload documents as PDFs under 5 MB.
  5. Pay via Remita, card or bank transfer.
  6. Receive the certificate.
  - Source: [Global Law Experts](https://globaladvisoryexperts.com/ndpc-data-controller-registration/)

### Inferences
- At launch HomeAI likely starts as OHL (₦10,000, annual renewal) and grows to EHL or UHL quickly. Count data subjects across tenants, landlords, agents and applicants, plus guarantors and referees named in applications.
- Monitor rolling 6-month volumes, and re-register or notify a tier change within 60 days.

### Gaps
- The current official fee schedule after the updated 2024/2025 notice and the GAID was not verified on ndpc.gov.ng. Commentary differs on whether registration fees rose.
- The OHL annual renewal fee under the GAID is not confirmed.

## 3. Cross-border data transfer (foreign cloud hosting, foreign LLM APIs)

### Takeaway
Hosting data abroad, or sending prompts with personal data to a foreign LLM API, is a cross-border transfer under NDPA ss. 41–43 and GAID Art. 45 / Schedule 5 **[IN FORCE]**. The transfer must rest on one of three grounds:
- an NDPC adequacy finding (no formal list has been published);
- a Cross-Border Data Transfer Instrument (SCCs, BCRs, codes or certifications), which some commentators say needs prior NDPC approval;
- a derogation such as informed consent or contract necessity.

A Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) should be documented.

### Cited Findings
- Transfers are permitted where the recipient country is adequate as assessed by the NDPC. Otherwise they need a CBDT instrument (codes of conduct, certifications, BCRs or SCCs) or a derogation: informed consent, contract performance, the data subject's sole benefit, public interest, legal claims or vital interests — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- Commentary locates the rules in NDPA ss. 41–43 and GAID Art. 45, with adequacy criteria in GAID Schedule 5. The NDPC has **not** published a formal adequacy list, and the NDPR-era whitelist has no continuing legal effect — [Legal500 / The Trusted Advisors](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/cross-border-data-transfers-an-overview-of-legal-requirements-and-compliance-mechanisms-in-nigeria/); [Adeola Oyinlade cross-border guide](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/?p=5690)
- One commentator says CBDT instruments, including SCCs, require **prior NDPC approval** before any transfer, so GDPR-style SCCs alone may not suffice — [Mondaq – cross-border transfers in global tech transactions](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1798234/cross-border-data-transfers-and-international-compliance-legal-opportunities-in-global-tech-transactions). Mondaq's GAID FAQ likewise says NDPC approval is required when relying on SCCs, and that TIAs are required — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1684204/unlocking-gaid-2025-answers-to-all-your-burning-questions)
- Using a cloud server outside Nigeria is a cross-border transfer — [ALN Africa](https://aln.africa/?p=7017)
- Consent is required for a cross-border transfer when consent is the basis relied on (GAID Art. 18) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- Cross-border transfer is itself a DPIA trigger — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025)

### Inferences
- For AWS, GCP or Azure and for OpenAI or Anthropic-type APIs:
  1. Sign processor DPAs with SCCs.
  2. Prepare a TIA per destination country.
  3. Consider asking the NDPC to approve the SCCs, or adopting any NDPC template once one is issued.
  4. Disclose transfers in the privacy notice and declare them on the DCPMI registration.
  5. Minimise what reaches the LLM: redact NIN, BVN and account numbers, and disable training or retention on the provider side.
- Relying only on user consent for routine hosting is fragile because consent can be withdrawn. Use consent as a backstop, not the primary transfer basis.
- No general data-localisation mandate for private property-tech platforms was found. The NITDA Nigerian Cloud Computing Policy and government-data localisation rules mainly affect public-sector data; these were not verified this session.

### Gaps
- No NDPC-approved SCC template or official adequacy list was found.
- Whether SCC approval is mandatory before any transfer, or only on request, is not settled in the sources.
- Exact NDPA section numbering (s. 41 vs s. 43 as the operative default rule) differs between commentators.

## 4. NIN/BVN for identity verification: NIMC rules, licensed partners, storage restrictions

### Takeaway
NIN checks must go through NIMC-licensed verification partners. NIMC moved to a tokenised **virtual NIN (vNIN)** flow designed so that verifiers do not retain the raw NIN. The **NIMC Act 2026**, signed in late June 2026, repealed the 2007 Act **[IN FORCE]**. It makes the NIN the foundational credential, makes NIMC the national PKI Root Certification Authority, and promises regulations to follow. BVN data is held by NIBSS and is mainly accessible to banks and financial institutions; non-banks reach it through licensed KYC aggregators. No official rule on BVN or NIN storage by non-banks was found, so data-minimisation principles should govern.

### Cited Findings
- NIMC provides NIN verification only through licensed partners. After the 2024 XpressVerify incident, NIMC suspended database access and said only licensees cleared on evidence of regulatory compliance may verify NINs. Licensees must be trained on the NDPA and NIMC's privacy policy — [TechEconomy](https://www.techeconomy.ng/nimc-denies-working-with-xpressverify-says-nin-database-uncompromised); [ITEdgeNews](https://www.itedgenews.africa/nimc-to-intensify-scrutiny-on-licensees-amid-data-protection-concerns/)

NIMC decrypts the token and returns results, and the vendor never sees the raw NIN. The change cut off third-party vendors on the old protocol (vendor source) — [Youverify](https://youverify.co/en/blogs/nimc-launches-virtual-nin-vnin)
- Most products access NIN and BVN checks through licensed third-party APIs that already hold NIMC or NIBSS access agreements (developer blog) — [Wise Hustlers](https://wise-hustlers.com/blog/nin-bvn-verification-integration-nigeria)
- The NIMC Act 2026 was signed by President Tinubu and repeals the 2007 Act:
  - It reinforces "one person, one identity".
  - It designates NIMC the Root Certification Authority for the national PKI.
  - It enables interoperable data exchange with private organisations.
  - It aligns safeguards with the NDPA.
  - It toughens penalties for multiple registration and identity fraud.
  - NIMC will issue regulations and guidelines later.
  - Sources: [ID Tech, 29 Jun 2026](https://idtechwire.com/nigeria-signs-nimc-act-2026-recasting-its-identity-commission-around-digital-trust/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/tinubu-signs-new-nimc-act-to-strengthen-identity-management/)

- TechCabal (13 Aug 2026) reports that the NIMC Act raises questions over digital signatures relative to the pending Digital Economy Bill. The article itself was blocked; only the search snippet was seen — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/)

Manual profile creation is not allowed — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/648175-updated-cbn-to-place-restrictions-on-accounts-without-nin-bvn.html)

### Inferences
- Use a NIMC-licensed provider and the vNIN flow. Store only the verification result, a timestamp and a provider reference, plus perhaps a masked or hashed identifier. Do not store raw NIN, BVN or the returned images longer than necessary; the GAID's six-month post-purpose deletion default applies.
- A face-match or liveness check processes biometric data, which is sensitive under the NDPA. Get explicit consent and cover it in the DPIA.
- A property platform has no legal mandate to collect BVN. Collecting it is hard to justify under data minimisation unless a payment or escrow partner needs it; NIN or other ID usually suffices for identity checks.

## 5. Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act 2024: service-provider obligations, ngCERT reporting, cybersecurity levy

### Takeaway
The Act was assented to on 28 February 2024 **[IN FORCE]**. It requires cyber incidents to be reported to the **sectoral CERT or SOC within 72 hours**, with a ₦2,000,000 fine for failure. It requires "service providers" to retain traffic and subscriber data for **2 years** in line with the NDPA and NCC rules. The 0.5% cybersecurity levy applies only to businesses in the Second Schedule (banks, financial institutions, insurers, ISPs and others), so it does **not** apply to HomeAI as a non-financial platform. Its CBN implementation circular was withdrawn on 17 May 2024 **[SUSPENDED]**.

### Cited Findings
- The reporting channel moved from the national CERT directly to **sectoral CERTs or SOCs**, and the deadline fell from 7 days to **72 hours** — [Templars ThoughtLab, 7 Aug 2024](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)
- Failure to report within 72 hours carries a mandatory ₦2,000,000 fine paid into the National Cybersecurity Fund — [TheNigeriaLawyer / search summary](https://thenigerialawyer.com/the-cybercrime-amendment-act-2024-key-provisions-and-innovations/)
- Service providers must keep and protect traffic data and subscriber information in line with the NDPA and as prescribed by the NCC, for **2 years** (s. 9 of the Amendment Act) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)
- The levy is 0.5% of electronic transaction value, imposed on businesses in the **Second Schedule**: ISPs, banks and other financial institutions, insurers, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and others. Defaulting is punishable by a fine of at least 2% of annual turnover and/or licence withdrawal — [Hamu Legal (via search)](https://hamulegal.com/highlights-of-cybercrimes-prohibition-prevention-etc-amendment-act-2024/); [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)
- The CBN implementation circular of 6 May 2024 was withdrawn by a CBN circular of 17 May 2024 after SERAP litigation and a House of Representatives resolution — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf); [Andersen Nigeria](https://ng.andersen.com/cybersecurity-levy-beyond-suspension-the-need-for-a-review-of-its-legality-and-timing/)
- The Presidency directed the NSA to suspend implementation — [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/tinubu-suspended-cybersecurity-levy-implementation-to-ease-economic-hardship-says-presidency)
- No 2026 report of the levy being reinstated was found (search, 26 Oct 2026).
- The Amendment allows e-signatures on previously excluded documents (wills, birth and death certificates and so on) if they are "legally verified in certified true copies". Templars calls the practical mechanics unclear — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Levy:** HomeAI is not in the Second Schedule, so it does not owe the levy. If it is ever revived, users' bank-originated payments could carry it, collected by the banks.
- **Data retention:** whether HomeAI is a "service provider" under the 2-year retention rule depends on the Act's definition (aimed at telecom and ISP-type providers). Conservatively, keep security logs (login, IP and access logs) for 2 years, within NDPA limits.

## 8. Electronic transactions: Evidence Act 2011 (as amended 2023), e-signed leases, Electronic Transactions / Digital Economy Bill

### Takeaway
E-signatures are generally binding under Cybercrimes Act s. 17, subject to listed exclusions. The Evidence (Amendment) Act 2023 lets electronic records satisfy "in writing" requirements (s. 84A), makes them admissible including from the cloud (s. 84B), and extends signature-proof rules to digital signatures (s. 93). Ordinary electronically signed tenancy agreements are therefore likely valid and admissible. Leases that must be made **by deed** and registered, typically terms over 3 years, are riskier. No comprehensive Electronic Transactions Act exists yet; the **National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill is still pending [BILL – NOT LAW]** as of the latest reports (July–August 2026).

### Cited Findings
- The Evidence (Amendment) Act 2023 amends ss. 84, 93, 108, 109, 110, 119, 255 and 258 of the Evidence Act 2011 — [Mondaq overview](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/arbitration-dispute-resolution/1465716/an-overview-of-the-evidence-amendment-act-2023); [LawPavilion](https://blog.lawpavilion.com/the-evidence-act-of-2023-a-remarkable-advancement-in-nigerias-jurisprudence)
- New s. 84A: where a law requires information to be in writing, the requirement is met if the information is in electronic form and accessible for later reference. New s. 84B: electronic records, including from cloud or database storage, are admissible without production of the original if conditions are met — [Omaplex](https://omaplex.com.ng/an-overview-of-the-evidence-amendment-act-2023/)
- Section 93 was extended to digital signatures. A digital signature must be proven to belong to the signer, unless it is a "secure" digital signature whose creation data was under the signer's exclusive control — [Omaplex](https://omaplex.com.ng/an-overview-of-the-evidence-amendment-act-2023/)
- Cybercrimes Act s. 17(1): electronic signatures are binding for all transactions unless specifically excluded. The s. 17(2) exclusions cover wills and testamentary documents, death and birth certificates, family-law matters, court documents, cancellation of utility services, and instruments relating to dangerous materials. Sources differ on the exact list, and **none mention leases** — [Omaplex](https://omaplex.com.ng/the-validity-and-limitation-of-electronic-signatures-under-the-nigerian-law); [Mondaq – Electronic signatures in Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/contracts-and-commercial-law/1314284/electronic-signatures-in-nigeria)
- The 2024 Amendment allows excluded documents to be e-signed if legally verified in certified true copies — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)
- **Digital Economy Bill status:**
  - The House of Representatives approved the National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill by May 2026, and it awaited the Senate — [TechCabal (snippet)](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/)
  - On 9 July 2026 the Senate ICT Committee chair said it was at third reading and would be ready "in weeks". It would give e-transactions and digital signatures the same legal weight as paper — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/09/national-digital-economy-and-e-governance-act-2026-ready-in-weeks/)
  - Earlier promises of passage by November 2025 and March 2026 were missed — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/11/10/tinubu-to-sign-landmark-digital-economy-bill-into-law-bosun-tijani/); [Legal Wire](https://thelegalwire.ai/nigeria-set-to-pass-ai-law-by-end-of-march-2026/)
- No report of Senate passage or presidential assent in September–October 2026 was found — [search, 26 Oct 2026; NASS bill tracker](https://nass.gov.ng/documents/bill/11219)
- The NIMC Act 2026 makes NIMC the national PKI Root Certification Authority, which is relevant to future "secure" digital signatures — [ID Tech](https://idtechwire.com/nigeria-signs-nimc-act-2026-recasting-its-identity-commission-around-digital-trust/)

### Inferences
- Short residential tenancies (yearly, or up to 3 years) signed through a reputable e-signature tool are likely enforceable. Capture an audit trail (identity-verified signer, IP and timestamps, document hash) so that s. 84 and s. 93 proof requirements can be met.
- For leases over 3 years, or where the lease must be registered or stamped under state Land Instruments Registration laws, get Nigerian counsel's view. Consider wet-ink or hybrid execution until the Digital Economy Bill passes.
- **Stamp duty:** tenancy agreements are dutiable instruments, and electronic documents are also within the Stamp Duties Act framework. This was not verified this session.

### Gaps
- The text of the s. 17(2) exclusions was not verified against the statute.
- State tenancy laws (for example, the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011) and Property and Conveyancing Law deed and registration requirements were not researched. They are out of scope here and may be covered elsewhere.
- Status of the Digital Economy Bill after August 2026 is unknown.

## 9. AI regulation: National AI Strategy, AI bills, NITDA guidance, NDPA on automated decision-making

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 Nigeria has **no standalone AI law [BILLS – NOT LAW]**. The **National AI Strategy 2025–2029**, published on 19 September 2025, is policy, not binding law. What binds HomeAI now is the data protection regime:
- **NDPA s. 37**: the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects;
- the **GAID**, which requires consent for automated decisions with legal effect, a DPIA before deploying AI, and documentation in the CAR.

The FCCPC's draft Guidance Note on AI chatbots signals coming disclosure duties. The pending Digital Economy Bill would make NITDA an AI regulator with risk classification and fines.

### Cited Findings
- NDPA s. 37(1) gives a right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that have legal or similarly significant effects. It is modelled on GDPR Art. 22, with exceptions for consent, contract necessity and legal authorisation — [Adeola Oyinlade – NDPA automated processing & AI](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/?p=2681); [Afriwise – Regulating AI in Nigeria](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/regulating-artificial-intelligence-in-nigeria)
- The GAID requires consent for automated decision-making with legal effect (Art. 18) — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://www.adeolaoyinlade.com/en/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc/)
- For emerging technologies including AI, the GAID requires a DPIA before deployment, sandbox testing, continuous monitoring, and documentation filed with the NDPC as part of the CAR — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG)
- Commentary says controllers deploying AI must consider the NDPA, the GAID and NDPC instruments, safeguards for sensitive data and vulnerable groups, cross-border flows and privacy by design. A DPIA is mandatory for AI-based personal data processing — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/regulating-artificial-intelligence-in-nigeria); [captaincompliance (via search)](https://captaincompliance.com/education/nigerias-data-protection-act-and-gaid-are-operationalizing-a-modern-data-privacy-regime/)
- The National AI Strategy 2025–2029 was published by the Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy on 19 September 2025 as a "living document". The August 2024 version was a draft — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/40784-federal-ministry-of-communication-innovation-and-digital-economy-released-national-artificial-intelligence-strategy); [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/policy-initiatives/national-artificial-intelligence-strategy-nais)
- As of August 2026 Nigeria had no standalone AI law. Several proposals were pending, including the National Artificial Intelligence Commission (Establishment) Bill and a Digital Sovereignty, AI Governance and Fair Compensation Bill — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/digest/nigeria-ai-governance-approach); [search summary of Tech In Africa / weetracker](https://weetracker.com/2026/01/13/nigeria-africa-ai-regulatory-law/)
- **Digital Economy Bill AI provisions [BILL]:**
  - NITDA would become the AI regulator, with powers to classify AI risk, mandate transparency and accredit AI auditors.
  - Higher-risk uses in finance, public administration and surveillance would need annual impact assessments.
  - Fines of up to ₦10m or 2% of an AI provider's annual gross revenue.
  - Licensing or registration before deploying AI systems.
  - Sources: [Bloomberg, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-13/nigeria-set-to-pass-sweeping-ai-rules-for-digital-economy); [weetracker](https://weetracker.com/2026/01/13/nigeria-africa-ai-regulatory-law/)
- **FCCPC draft Guidance Note No. 1/2026** covers AI chatbots and automated conversational agents, AI-generated testimonials and imagery, and internal AI governance for undertakings using AI in consumer communications **[DRAFT]** — [FCCPC Exposure Notice](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FCCPC-Consumer-Protection-Exposure-Notice.pdf)

### Inferences
- **AI-scored rental applications:** if HomeAI or a landlord rejects an applicant solely on an AI score, that is likely a "legal or similarly significant effect" decision. To comply:
  1. Make sure a human (landlord or agent) makes the final decision, so the decision is not solely automated.
  2. Otherwise, obtain explicit consent and offer a right to contest, human review and an explanation.
  3. Carry out a DPIA.
  4. Test the model for bias, for example on gender, ethnicity or proxy variables such as location or employer.
  5. Exclude sensitive data such as religion, ethnicity and health from the model.
- **LLM chat:**
  - disclose that the assistant is AI;
  - don't feed NIN, BVN or financial documents to a foreign model without a transfer basis and a DPA;
  - log outputs;
  - add a disclaimer that it is not legal or financial advice;
  - avoid AI-generated property images without labelling (FCCPC draft).
- If the Digital Economy Bill passes in its reported form, AI-based credit or tenant scoring could be classed as "higher-risk (finance)". Build a risk register and impact assessment now so it can be repurposed.

### Gaps
- No NITDA AI-specific guideline or framework issued in 2026 was found.
- The final text of the Digital Economy Bill's AI chapter, and its effective date if enacted, were not available.
