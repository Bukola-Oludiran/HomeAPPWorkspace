# Employment Law and Statutory Contributions for HomeAI (Nigeria), as of October 2026

Scope: obligations of a Nigerian employer (HomeAI, a prop-tech startup) hiring engineers, operations and customer-support staff, and engaging gig-style field agents for inspections, cleaning and repairs. Federal law, plus Lagos specifics where found. Research date: 26 Oct 2026.

Source-quality note: the most authoritative source used is the Ikeyi Shittu & Co Nigeria chapter in Global Legal Insights (GLI) Employment & Labour Law 2025 (published June 2025), cited below as "GLI 2025". Many 2026 figures come from HR/payroll-vendor blogs (AnooreHR, WadataHR, WhichPayroll and others). They are current but secondary, and they sometimes conflict, so conflicts are flagged. I did not fetch the official statute texts (Labour Act Cap L1 LFN 2004, PRA 2014, ECA 2010, ITF Act, NHF Act, NTA 2025), so section numbers taken from secondary sources should be checked against the statutes before anyone relies on them.

## 1. Labour Act: coverage, written terms, hours, leave, termination; reform status

### Takeaway
In practice the Labour Act protects only "workers": manual or clerical staff, which excludes people with administrative, executive, technical or professional functions. HomeAI's engineers and most managers therefore fall mainly under contract and common law. Junior ops and support staff, and possibly field agents, may be covered. The Act sets low minimums: written terms within 3 months, 6 working days' annual leave after 12 months, 12 days' paid sick leave, 12 weeks' maternity leave at 50% or more of pay, and notice of 1 day to 1 month. The NICN increasingly adds an "international best practice" overlay requiring a valid reason and fair process. The Labour Standards Bill, pending since 2008, had still not passed as of the latest reports I found (2025). I found no 2026 status.

### Cited Findings
**Coverage / definition of "worker" (s.91)**
- Labour Act definition: a "worker" is "any person who has entered into or works under a contract with an employer, whether the contract is for manual labour or clerical work... expressed or implied or oral or written, and whether it is a contract of service or a contract personally to execute any work or labour". Excluded are: persons employed otherwise than for the employer's business; persons exercising administrative, executive, technical or professional functions; family members; "representatives, agents and commercial travellers"; "any person to whom articles or materials are given out to be made or cleaned"; and persons employed in a vessel or aircraft. The Act does not define "employee". — [GLI 2025, Ikeyi Shittu & Co](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Labour Act "workers" are generally the so-called "unskilled" or "junior" staff. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Exception: in *Ekwelem Chinenye v Acron Medical Consultant Ltd* (NICN/YEN/25/2017, 3 May 2019), the NICN held that the Labour Act's maternity provisions apply to all categories of employees, not only Labour Act "workers". — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- For employees outside the Act, leave and notice are matters of contract, but the Labour Act "provide[s] a guide". — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

**Written terms (s.7)**
- The employer must give a written statement of terms no later than 3 months after employment begins. Contents: the parties, nature of the job, any fixed-term expiry date, notice period, wages and payment intervals, hours, holidays and sick pay. — [Mondaq overview of employment laws](https://webiis05.mondaq.com/Nigeria/Employment-and-HR/763172/Overview-Of-Employment-Related-Laws-In-Nigeria); [WageIndicator Nigeria](https://wageindicator.org/en-ng/work-in-nigeria/labour-law/contracts-and-dismissals)
- Section 7(2): terms may be changed on reasonable notice. NICN case law (*Ekeoma Ajah v Fidelity Bank*, NICN/LA/588/2017, 2019) suggests employee agreement is effectively required. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Unverified claim: one LinkedIn post says probation cannot exceed 3 months. Statutory support was not found. — [WageIndicator](https://wageindicator.org/en-ng/work-in-nigeria/labour-law/contracts-and-dismissals) describes the 3-month window as treated "in practice" as implied probation.
- Section 8: mandatory medical examination at the employer's expense for every worker entering a contract, subject to the exemptions in s.8(2). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

**Working hours (s.13)**
- Normal hours are fixed by mutual agreement, by collective bargaining, or by an industrial wages board. The Act sets no general statutory maximum. A worker at work for 6 or more hours a day must get rest interval(s) totalling at least 1 hour (s.13(3)). There is no statutory overtime pay; overtime, bonus and commission are matters of contract or CBA. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

**Leave**
- Annual leave (s.18): at least 6 working days on full pay after 12 months' service; 12 working days for workers under 16 and apprentices. Leave can be deferred by agreement, but the earning period cannot exceed 24 months. Pro-rata holiday pay is due if the worker is dismissed before 12 months. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Sick leave (s.16): up to 12 working days' paid sick leave per calendar year. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Maternity (s.54): 12 weeks, with no work permitted in the 6 weeks after confinement, at not less than 50% of wages if the employee has 6 or more months' continuous service. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Paternity: no Labour Act right. The public sector gives federal civil servants 14 days and Lagos civil servants 2 weeks (first two children). Private employers are not bound but are trending toward 4–6 months' maternity and some paternity leave. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Dismissing a woman during pregnancy or confinement absence, or for trade union membership, is "automatically unfair" (*Maiya v Clinton Health Access Initiative* [2012]). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

**Termination / notice (s.11) and dismissal case law**
- Statutory minimum notice for Labour Act workers by length of service:
  - 3 months or less: 1 day
  - over 3 months and under 2 years: 1 week
  - 2 to 5 years: 2 weeks
  - 5 years or more: 1 month
  - Longer notice may be agreed. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Redundancy (s.20): the employer must inform the union or workers' representative of the reasons for and extent of the redundancy. "Last in, first out" applies to Labour Act workers, subject to relative merit. Severance is negotiated. The NICN may import ILO C158 consultation as best practice even though Nigeria has not ratified C158. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Valid reason: *Aloysius v Diamond Bank* [2015] 58 NLLR (Pt.199) 92 requires a valid reason for termination, relying on ILO C158 as international best practice under s.254C(1)(f) of the Constitution. *Emana Edet v Fidelity Bank* (2019) says that best practice must be pleaded and proved. If any reason is given, the employer must justify it (*Adejo v Arksego*, 2020). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Probation: in *Adefisoye Stephen Abiodun v Safari Support West Ltd* (NICN/LA/347/2023, 17 Sept 2024), the court held that even a termination during probation needs a plausible justification if it is disputed. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Pay in lieu of notice should be paid on the effective termination date (*Kayode Tijani v FRA Williams*, 2019). Supreme Court authority is mixed. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Example damages: indefinite suspension without pay amounts to constructive dismissal (*Olasumbo v Ecobank*, 2017). Awards have included ₦20m general damages (*Ehigie v First Bank*, 2021) and ₦7.5m (*Philip v Notore*, 2022). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Restrictive covenants are prima facie void unless reasonable. FCCPA 2018 s.68(1)(e) caps non-competes at 2 years. The NICN applies the Tillman severance approach. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Equal pay: in *Uzo Ejekwumadu v Blue Arrow TSW Ltd* (NICN/LA/242/2016, 2021), pay disparity between local and foreign staff was held discriminatory and an unfair labour practice. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Trade unions: junior staff are automatically members of the relevant union unless they opt out in writing. Senior staff must opt in. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

**Reform status**
- The Labour Standards Bill (which includes "equal pay for equal work") was submitted to the National Assembly in 2008 and "is yet to be passed into law" as of mid-2025. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Feb 2025: the Labour Ministry said it had amended the Bill's definition of "worker" to include domestic workers and would forward the reviewed Bill to the National Assembly. — [allAfrica, Feb 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202502170036.html)
- May 2025: the Minister of Labour urged the House to expedite the Labour Standards Bill, the Collective Labour Relations Bill and the Occupational Safety and Health Bill. — [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/fg-asks-nass-to-expedite-passage-of-key-labour-bills/); [allAfrica, May 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202505130064.html)
- Dec 2025: the House held a public hearing on an Occupational Safety and Health Bill 2025. — [allAfrica, Dec 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512120070.html)
- Mar 2025: a private-member Domestic Workers bill passed second reading in the House. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- In 2024 the ILO described the Labour Act as insufficient for modern workforce protection. — [Nairametrics, Nov 2024](https://nairametrics.com/2024/11/13/nigerias-labour-act-insufficient-for-modern-workforce-protection-ilo/)

### Inferences
- Engineers and professional staff are outside the Labour Act's "worker" definition, so their contracts should set out hours, leave, notice and benefits explicitly, using the Act as a floor.
- Junior customer-support and operations staff doing clerical work are likely Labour Act "workers". Section 7 written terms, s.11 notice, s.18 and s.16 leave, the s.8 medical exam and union opt-out rules apply to them.
- Maternity protections should be applied to all female staff (per *Chinenye*).
- As best practice, document a valid reason and a fair-hearing process for every termination, including during probation.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed the Labour Standards Bill's status in 2026 (committee stage, passage or assent). Check the National Assembly bill tracker.
- The exact statutory wording of s.7 (including any exemption where a full written contract already exists) was not verified against the Act text.
- The Labour Act's general penalty amounts for breaches were not found.

## 2. National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Act 2024

### Takeaway
The national minimum is ₦70,000 per month, in force since 1 May 2024 (signed 29 July 2024). Employers with fewer than 25 workers are exempt, as are part-time, commission, piece-rate and seasonal workers. The review cycle is now 3 years. In October 2026 the Federal Government said the next review is due on 1 June 2027, while the NLC is demanding it start now. No new figure has been set.

### Cited Findings
- The 2024 Amendment Act raised the minimum wage from ₦30,000 to ₦70,000 per month (133% increase). It was signed on 29 July 2024, effective retroactively from 1 May 2024, and shortened the review cycle from 5 to 3 years. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Exemptions:
  - employers with fewer than 25 workers
  - part-time, commission-paid or piece-rate workers
  - seasonal (e.g. agricultural) workers
  - workers on vessels or aircraft
  - The Minister may add further exemptions. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- 12 Oct 2026: Labour Minister Muhammad Dingyadi told labour that the wage expires and becomes due for review on 1 June 2027, calling an early review premature. — [ThisDay, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/10/16/minimum-wage-review-due-in-june-2027-fg-tells-labour/)
- The NLC (Joe Ajaero, 13 Oct 2026) rejected that timeline and demanded the tripartite committee be inaugurated now. No new amount has been agreed. — [WithinNigeria, 18 Oct 2026](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/10/18/new-minimum-wage-in-nigeria-latest-update-on-nlcs-demands-and-fgs-response-october-2026/); [The Sun](https://thesun.ng/nlc-rejects-fgs-three-year-minimum-wage-review-timeline/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/nlc-pushes-for-2026-review-of-minimum-wage-amid-rising-living-costs/)
- June 2026: Chief of Staff Femi Gbajabiamila said the wage must be reassessed, without giving a timetable. — [allAfrica, June 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202606260042.html)

### Inferences
- Below 25 workers, HomeAI is technically exempt, but paying at least ₦70,000 is advisable. Once it reaches 25 workers, the ₦70,000 floor applies to the lowest-paid worker.
- Field agents paid per job (piece rate or commission) would fall in an exempt category even if reclassified as workers. This is relevant but untested.
- Budget for a possible upward revision from mid-2027.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the penalty for paying below the minimum wage under the 2019 Act as amended in 2024.
- It is unclear how the "25 workers" headcount treats contractors.

## 3. Pension Reform Act 2014 (PRA): contributions, threshold, PenCom certificate, group life

### Takeaway
Minimum contributions are 10% from the employer and 8% from the employee (18% total) of monthly emoluments (basic + housing + transport). They must be remitted to the employee's PFC/PFA within 7 working days of paying salary. Late payment carries a penalty of at least 2% per month, paid into the employee's RSA. The employee threshold is commonly given as 3 or more, but sources conflict. Covered employers must also maintain group life insurance of at least 3x annual total emoluments. A Pension Clearance (Compliance) Certificate is needed for government contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Rates: employee at least 8%, employer at least 10%, combined at least 18%. The employer may choose to bear the whole cost but cannot go below the floors. The base is basic, housing and transport ("monthly emoluments"), not gross pay. — [AnooreHR, 5 Jul 2026](https://anoorehr.com/blog/pension-remittance-nigeria-pencom-deadlines-2026)
- Remittance: deduct at source and remit within 7 working days of paying salary, to the PFC named by the employee's PFA. One outlier says 7 working days after month-end, which conflicts. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/pension-remittance-nigeria-pencom-deadlines-2026); [Pension Nigeria FAQ](https://www.pensionnigeria.com/faq/5-what-is-the-punishment-for-an-employer-who-fails-to-remit-pension-contribution/); contrary: [EmploySome](https://employsome.com/hire/nigeria/pencom/)
- Penalty for late remittance: at least 2% of unpaid contributions for each month or part-month of default, recoverable as a debt and paid into the employee's RSA. Secondary sources put it at s.11(6). — [Pension Nigeria FAQ](https://www.pensionnigeria.com/faq/5-what-is-the-punishment-for-an-employer-who-fails-to-remit-pension-contribution/); [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/pension-remittance-nigeria-pencom-deadlines-2026)
- Enforcement: PenCom uses recovery agents and prosecutes persistent defaulters. — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/pencom-insight-recovering-unremitted-pension-contributions-and-the-task-before-pencom/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/double-whammy-as-pension-defaulters-cough-up-n24bn/)
- Threshold (conflicting): a secondary guide says the scheme applies to private employers with 3 or more employees, while another says 15 or more. The AnooreHR 2026 guide gives no number and advises confirming with PenCom. — search summary of [NNRoad 2026 guide](https://nnroad.com/compliance/nigeria/) and [EORHQ 2026 guide](https://eorhq.com/guides/nigeria/); [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/pension-remittance-nigeria-pencom-deadlines-2026)
- Group life insurance (s.4(5)): every covered employer must maintain a policy for each employee worth at least 3x the employee's annual total emolument (gross emoluments, per the s.120 definition). The premium is paid by the employer and is separate from RSA contributions. — [PenCom Revised GLIP Guidelines 2020](https://www.pencom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Revised-Guideline-on-GLIP-2020.pdf); [OAL](https://oal.law/legal-consequence-of-non-compliance-with-group-life-insurance-provison-of-the-pension-reform-act/)
- If there is no cover and an employee dies in service, the employer must pay the beneficiaries the equivalent of the insurance benefit (about 300% of gross emolument). The general offence provision cited is a fine of at least ₦250,000, or at least 1 year's imprisonment, or both. — [OAL](https://oal.law/legal-consequence-of-non-compliance-with-group-life-insurance-provison-of-the-pension-reform-act/); [allAfrica, PenCom to enforce GLIP](https://allafrica.com/stories/202001240756.html)
- A current GLIP certificate is required to obtain a PenCom Pension Clearance Certificate, which is in turn needed for most government contract bids. — [Yoa Insurance Brokers](https://yoainsurance.com/group-life-insurance-requirements-nigeria/) (broker source, secondary)
- NAICOM has published "Revised Guidelines on Group Life Insurance Policy for Employees" (uploaded July 2026). I did not review its contents. — [NAICOM PDF](https://naicom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Revised-Guidelines-on-Group-Life-Insurance-Policy-for-Employees.pdf)
- PenCom lets employees report employer non-compliance directly. — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/pension-insight-pencom-empowers-employees-to-report-non-compliance/)

### Inferences
- HomeAI should register with PenCom, ensure every employee has an RSA with a PFA, and remit monthly within 7 working days of payroll from the first hires. The 3-employee threshold will almost certainly be crossed early.
- Buy group life cover at 3x annual emoluments from hire.
- Get a Pension Clearance Certificate if HomeAI may bid for government or public-sector property contracts.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the employee-count threshold or the PCC section (often cited as s.41) against the PRA 2014 text. PenCom's own site was not fetched.
- I did not review the contents or changes of the July 2026 NAICOM revised GLIP guideline.
- No 2025–2026 PenCom circular changing rates was found.
- I did not confirm the treatment of expatriates (the PRA exemption for those covered by a home-country scheme).

## 4. Employees' Compensation Act 2010 / NSITF

### Takeaway
The employer alone pays at least 1% of total monthly payroll to NSITF, with no headcount threshold (one employee is enough). Sources differ on the monthly deadline (10th vs. before the 16th of the following month) and on the penalty (a flat 10% of the unpaid amount vs. 5–10% per month). NTA 2025 did not change the ECA levy.

### Cited Findings
- Section 33 ECA: minimum contribution of 1% of total monthly payroll, paid by the employer only (no employee deduction). It applies to every employer with at least one employee. — [WadataHR, updated 19 Oct 2026](https://www.wadatahr.com/guides/nsitf); [AnooreHR NSITF/ITF 2026](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)
- Base: gross pay including basic, allowances, overtime and bonuses. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)
- Registration: register with NSITF on incorporation once there are any employees. Keep remittance receipts for 6 or more years. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)
- Deadline (conflicting): "typically by the 10th of the following month", with a recommendation to confirm with NSITF ([AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)), vs. "before the 16th day of the succeeding month" (a payroll guide in the same search results: [MyWorkPay](https://www.myworkpay.com/blogs/statutory-deductions-in-nigeria-a-comprehensive-overview-and-detailed-guide) / [SIAO](https://siao.ng/detailed-guide-to-statutory-contributions-in-nigeria/)).
- Penalty (conflicting): s.46 allows NSITF to add 10% of the unpaid assessment, while NSITF's site describes 5–10% per month in default ([WadataHR](https://www.wadatahr.com/guides/nsitf)). AnooreHR says 10% of unpaid contributions plus daily interest, uncapped ([AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)).
- One guide lists a 0.5% employee contribution. Other sources treat the levy as employer-only. — search summary of [Africarrieres](https://africarrieres.com/nigeria/en/guide/employeur-entreprise/employer-taxes) (treat as unreliable)
- NTA 2025 (effective 1 Jan 2026) left NSITF and ITF as standalone levies under their own Acts. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)

### Inferences
- Register with NSITF from the first hire and remit 1% of gross monthly payroll by the 10th to be safe.
- Engaging contractors does not attract ECA contributions, but reclassification would trigger back-contributions plus penalties.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the NSITF deadline or penalty against the ECA text or nsitf.gov.ng.
- I did not research the NSITF compliance certificate and its role in procurement.

## 5. Industrial Training Fund (ITF) Act (as amended 2011)

### Takeaway
Employers with 5 or more employees or annual turnover of ₦50m or more must register with the ITF and pay 1% of annual payroll, typically by 1 April for the prior year. Up to 50% can be reimbursed for approved training. The late-payment penalty is reported as 5% per month. One source gives a conflicting threshold of 25 or more employees or ₦50m payroll.

### Cited Findings
- Threshold: 5 or more employees OR annual turnover of at least ₦50,000,000; either condition triggers liability. Rate: 1% of annual gross payroll, paid by the employer. Deadline: typically by 1 April for the preceding year. Up to 50% is refundable for approved training. Penalty: 5% of the unpaid levy per month of default. — [AnooreHR NSITF/ITF 2026](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026); [WhichPayroll](https://whichpayroll.com/countries/nigeria/contractor-management)
- Conflict: one source in the search gives 25 or more employees or ₦50m payroll. Others give 5 or more or ₦50m turnover. — [WadataHR/related search results](https://www.wadatahr.com/guides/nsitf)
- Levy-paying employers must train indigenous staff, submit training evidence to the ITF Governing Council, and accept students for industrial attachment (SIWES). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- A 2024 notice reportedly made ITF compliance a precondition for some federal contracts and export clearances. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026) (unverified)
- NTA 2025 did not fold the ITF into the new 4% Development Levy. — [AnooreHR](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nsitf-itf-employer-obligations-2026)

### Inferences
- HomeAI will likely trigger the ITF at its 5th employee. Register then, budget 1% of annual payroll, and keep training records to claim the up-to-50% refund.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the 2011 Amendment Act text (threshold wording, penalty section).
- I did not confirm whether the threshold is ₦50m turnover vs. payroll, or whether contractor payments count.

## 6. National Housing Fund (NHF): rules, status and controversy

### Takeaway
The NHF is 2.5% of basic salary, deducted from the employee (not an employer cost) and remitted by the employer to FMBN. Several sources say private-sector participation is now voluntary, but they disagree on which law changed it: the Business Facilitation Act 2022, s.45 (amending NHF Act s.4), or the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective 1 Jan 2026). Some guides still describe it as mandatory. HomeAI should deduct only with an employee's documented opt-in.

### Cited Findings
- Historic rule: mandatory 2.5% of basic salary for employees earning ₦3,000 or more a month under the NHF Act 1992. From 1 Jan 2026 it is voluntary for private-sector employees under NTA 2025 and mandatory only for federal public-service staff. The rate is unchanged. Employers should remit only for opted-in staff to FMBN, and continuing to deduct after an opt-out is an "unauthorised deduction". No NTA section number is cited. — [AnooreHR, 19 Apr 2026](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nhf-voluntary-opt-out-2026)
- Contrary legal basis: Business Facilitation (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2022 (signed Feb 2023), s.45, amended s.4 of the NHF Act so that private-sector employees "are no longer mandated" and may contribute voluntarily. Refunds are formally available only at age 60, on retirement or on incapacity, and FMBN guidance on opt-out refunds was awaited. — [Pension Nigeria, 20 May 2023](https://www.pensionnigeria.com/pension-news/national-housing-fund-nhf-deduction-no-longer-compulsory-for-private-sector-employees-how-do-you-get-your-refund/)
- NHF contributions are deductible in computing taxable income under NTA 2025, s.30(2)(a)(i). — [Mondaq, PIT deductions under NTA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/tax-authorities/1751764/comprehensive-guide-personal-income-tax-deductions-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025)
- Outdated or conflicting: some 2026 guides still call the NHF mandatory for all employees, with a 5% late-remittance penalty. — [Africarrieres](https://africarrieres.com/nigeria/en/guide/employeur-entreprise/employer-taxes); [SmartSMS](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/ng/statutory-deductions-payroll-pension-nhf-emtl-nigeria)

### Inferences
- Treat the NHF as employee-elective. Record each hire's election in the offer letter, deduct and remit only for opt-ins, and keep evidence. Unauthorised deductions risk claims.
- Both sources point to "voluntary", so the practical result is the same whichever law is the true basis. The legal basis matters only for arguments about arrears before 2026.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the BFA 2022 s.45 wording or the NTA 2025 provision against the official texts.
- The current FMBN position, the remittance deadline for opt-ins, and any pending NHF (Amendment) Bill were not found.

## 7. Employee vs independent contractor; gig-platform field agents; NICN case law

### Takeaway
Nigeria has no gig-worker statute. Courts look at substance over labels, using tests of control, integration, own tools, payment method, right to delegate, and economic dependency. The leading platform case is *Olatunji & John v Uber & Taxify* (NICN, 2018). It was dismissed for insufficient evidence, not on the merits, and was reportedly appealed; I found no appellate ruling. The Labour Act's "worker" definition explicitly includes a "contract personally to execute any work or labour", which creates real reclassification risk for field agents who personally perform inspections, cleaning and repairs under platform control.

### Cited Findings
- Labour Act "worker" covers a "contract of service or a contract personally to execute any work or labour". It excludes "representatives, agents and commercial travellers" and persons to whom articles are "given out to be made or cleaned". — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Contractors operate under a contract for services, so the Labour Act does not protect them. Their remedies lie in breach of contract (*Shena Security Co. Ltd v Afropak (Nig.) Ltd* (2008) 18 NWLR (Pt.1118) 77 (SC); *Iyere v Bendel Feed & Flour Mill* (2008)). However, the NICN "will look at the substance of the relationship" and extend employee-level anti-discrimination and retaliation protection where the substance is employment. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Tests: control; integration into core operations; who supplies tools; salary-like vs. per-project payment; right to delegate; economic dependency (said to be weighted more heavily now). Contract labels are not determinative. — [WhichPayroll, Apr 2026](https://whichpayroll.com/countries/nigeria/contractor-management) (affiliate-funded, secondary)
- Misclassification exposure: back pay and benefits; 10% employer and 8% employee pension contributions plus PenCom penalties; NSITF at 1%; ITF at 1%; back PAYE with penalties from state revenue authorities; and failure to deduct withholding tax (WHT) on service fees. — [WhichPayroll](https://whichpayroll.com/countries/nigeria/contractor-management)
- *Oladapo Olatunji & Daniel John v Uber Technologies System Nigeria Ltd & Ors* (NICN/LA/546/2017, Kanyip J., 4 Dec 2018) was a class action seeking a declaration that drivers were employees. It was dismissed because the claimants did not prove a master-servant relationship. The court acknowledged that the traditional worker/employer distinction has been "stretched" and that judicial or legislative action is needed. — [Perchstone & Graeys newsletter, Nov 2019](https://perchstoneandgraeys.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Employment-Law-Newsletter-Nov-2019.pdf); [Mondaq, Kevin Martin Ogwemoh Legal, Mar 2023](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/contract-of-employment/1299322/gig-economy-workers-employees-or-independent-contractors)
- The drivers argued that Uber set conditions and assigned rides (control). Uber argued drivers were independent businesses. — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=378682)
- In 2023 the drivers' union said the matter was before the Court of Appeal and undetermined. Uber and Bolt also challenged the standing of the drivers' union (AUATWON) under the Trade Unions Act. — [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/?p=135837); [TechCabal, Apr 2023](https://techcabal.com/2023/04/19/uber-and-bolt-vs-drivers-union-are-drivers-employees-or-independent-contractors/); [TechCabal, Jul 2023](https://techcabal.com/2023/07/01/despite-pushback-from-bolt-and-uber-nigerias-ride-hailing-union-unites-against-unfavourable-working-terms/)
- A 2025 academic analysis confirms the dismissal was on evidence and that the classification question remains unsettled. — [SSRN, Adeyemo](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6181279)
- Platforms that set standards, inspect workers and control pricing and payment risk being treated as employers. Commentators recommend careful drafting and portable-benefit models. — [Mondaq, 2023](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/contract-of-employment/1299322/gig-economy-workers-employees-or-independent-contractors); [Manifield Solicitors](https://manifieldsolicitors.com/regulating-platform-work-in-nigeria-workers-economic-rights-and-obligations-in-the-gig-economy/)
- WHT on contractor service fees: 5% for individuals, 10% for companies, remitted monthly to FIRS (now NRS). The source does not reference NTA 2025. — [WhichPayroll](https://whichpayroll.com/countries/nigeria/contractor-management)

### Inferences
- Higher risk factors for HomeAI field agents: HomeAI sets prices and pays through the platform; quality standards are enforced through ratings or deactivation; mandatory uniforms or branded kits; set shifts; exclusivity; no right to substitute; dependence on HomeAI for income.
- Lower risk factors: agents set their own availability and can decline jobs; they supply their own tools; they can work for competitors; they can substitute helpers; they invoice per job or have their own business registration.
- Even reclassified as "workers", per-job agents would likely fall in the commission/piece-rate exemption from the minimum wage. The pension, NSITF and ITF exposures would remain.
- Cleaners might be argued to fall under the Labour Act exclusion for persons to whom "articles or materials are given out to be... cleaned". That exclusion targets outworkers, not on-site cleaners, so HomeAI should not rely on it.
- Practical steps: a clear contract-for-services; let agents decline and substitute; avoid employer-style discipline; withhold and remit WHT; keep a robust record of agent autonomy.

### Gaps
- No NICN or Court of Appeal decision from 2024–2026 on platform or gig worker classification was found. The Court of Appeal outcome of the Uber/Taxify appeal is unknown.
- The NICN line on casualisation and outsourcing (triangular employment) was not researched.
- I did not verify WHT rates for contractor fees under NTA 2025 and the new Nigeria Revenue Service regulations.

## 8. Expatriate employment (quota, permits, levy)

### Takeaway
Hiring foreigners requires a Business Permit/Expatriate Quota from the Federal Ministry of Interior, processed since 1 May 2025 on a digital Expatriate Administration System, plus a visa (STR) and CERPAC residence card. The understudy (Nigerian trainee) rule is now strictly enforced. The 2024 Expatriate Employment Levy (US$15,000 per director and US$10,000 per other expatriate per year) remains suspended according to the US State Department's 2026 report.

### Cited Findings
- 1 May 2025: the Ministry of Interior and NIS launched reforms covering a digital Expatriate Administration System for new and renewed quota applications, comprehensive authenticity checks, a strict understudy policy (one source says at least 2 Nigerians trained per expatriate), penalties extended to licence revocation, repatriation costs shifted to expatriates, and no amnesty. — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/nigerian-immigration-update-major-reforms-in-expatriate-administration-and-visa-regime); [KPMG Nigeria, Apr 2025](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2025/04/Federal%20Ministry%20of%20Interior%20Announces%20Major%20Reforms%20in%20Expatriate%20Administration%20and%20Visa%20Policy.pdf); [Lexpraxis](https://lexpraxisng.com/new-reforms-on-expatriate-quota-administration-in-nigeria/)
- The Expatriate Employment Levy was introduced in February 2024 (handbook launched 27 Feb 2024): US$15,000 per year per director-expatriate and US$10,000 per other expatriate, deductible as a business expense. It was due to start on 15 March 2024 but was suspended on 8 March 2024. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- 2026: the levy "remains suspended". The government has strengthened and digitised standard quota administration. — [US State Dept, 2026 Investment Climate Statement: Nigeria](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/nigeria)
- Fees reported: ₦1,000,000 for quota establishment and ₦500,000 for renewal. An upward fee revision has been mooted but not confirmed. — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/here-are-8-visa-reforms-expatriates-in-nigeria-you-need-to-know/)
- Commentary (non-official) says quota returns are being cross-checked against CERPAC issuances in 2026. — [Viopat Consultants, Dec 2025](https://viopatconsultants.com/2025/12/31/nigerian-expatriate-quota-audit-2026-compliance/)
- Pay disparity between expatriate and local staff in equal roles can be an unfair labour practice (*Ejekwumadu v Blue Arrow*, 2021). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

### Inferences
- Any foreign hire needs a business permit, a quota position, an understudy plan, STR/CERPAC and monthly expatriate returns. Budget the potential US$10,000–15,000 levy as a contingent cost in case the suspension is lifted.

### Gaps
- Official Ministry pages could not be fetched (the clarification page returned 404). Current quota fees, minimum share-capital conditions and monthly return deadlines were not verified.

## 9. Remote/foreign-based staff and employee data (NDPA)

### Takeaway
I found little authority specific to remote staff. Nigerian data law, however, clearly applies to employee data: the NDPA 2023 and the NDPC's GAID 2025 (in force from 20 March 2025) replace the NDPR. Cross-border transfers of HR data (e.g., to foreign HR or payroll SaaS) need an NDPA s.41–43 basis. The old adequacy whitelist cannot be relied on.

### Cited Findings
- The NDPA is the substantive data-protection law. The GAID 2025, issued 20 March 2025, says the NDPC will no longer apply the NDPR 2019 (art. 3(3)), and the NDPR Implementation Framework has likely lapsed too. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Lawful bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public task, or legitimate interest. Employees are protected data subjects. Using employee data beyond the contract or sharing it with third parties requires consultation and consent. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Cross-border transfers (s.41–43 NDPA) are allowed on adequacy, binding corporate rules, contractual clauses, codes or certification, or on derogations (consent, contract necessity, and others). The NITDA whitelist was nullified (*Ikigai Innovation Institute v NITDA*, FHC, 28 Nov 2023), and GAID art. 45(2) and Schedule 5 now govern adequacy assessments. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Monitoring: official email, internet, phone use and CCTV may be monitored. Private communications may not, as that breaches s.37 of the Constitution. Background checks must disclose their purpose. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- HIV testing may only happen at the employee's instance (s.10 HIV & AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act 2014; *Akinola v Ocean Marine Solutions*, NICN/LA/410/2019, 2021). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Remote work is spreading but not universal, and there is some rollback over productivity concerns. In March 2025 the Federal Government announced a partnership with foreign governments on remote work for Nigerian youth. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- The NHIA Act 2022 health-insurance mandate is "live", but contribution rates are not yet standardised. — [WhichPayroll](https://whichpayroll.com/countries/nigeria/contractor-management) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Staff based abroad will mostly be governed by their host country's employment and tax law. An EOR or contractor model is common, but no Nigerian source was found on this.
- For HR systems hosted abroad, document the NDPA transfer basis (contract necessity plus contractual safeguards).
- Field-agent identity and location data processed by the platform is also personal data under the NDPA. HomeAI should check whether it qualifies as a data controller/processor of major importance (registration and fees). Other researchers' notes may cover this.

### Gaps
- There is no Nigerian authority on whether Nigerian employment statutes (PRA, ECA, Labour Act) apply to employees working from abroad for a Nigerian company, or on PAYE residency treatment under NTA 2025.
- NDPC registration thresholds and fees for HR data were not researched here.

## 10. Workplace policies required by law (harassment, HIV/AIDS, disability, safety; Lagos)

### Takeaway
There is no general federal sexual-harassment statute for private employers. The NICN Rules (Order 14) define harassment, Nigeria has ratified ILO C190, and employers who fail to investigate are liable, so a written policy is effectively necessary. The HIV & AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act 2014 reportedly requires employers with 5 or more employees to adopt a written HIV workplace policy within 12 months and lodge it with the Labour Minister. The Disability Act 2018 prohibits discrimination (fines of ₦1m for companies). Its 5% employment quota is generally read as applying to public organisations. In Lagos, the Special People's Law 2011 and the Lagos State Safety Commission regime apply.

### Cited Findings
- Sexual harassment: there are no specialised statutory rules. NICN Rules 2017 Order 14 r.1 defines physical, verbal, non-verbal and quid pro quo harassment. State criminal codes criminalise sexual harassment. Nigeria has ratified ILO C190 (Violence and Harassment Convention 2019). — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Employers that fail to investigate are liable: *Ejike Maduka v Microsoft* (2014) 41 NLLR (Pt.125) 67; *Dorothy Awogu v TFG Real Estate Ltd* (NICN/LA/262/2013, 2018, ₦2.5m damages, notably a real-estate employer). Commentators advise a written policy with reporting, investigation and sanctions. NDAs cannot bar harassment reporting. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Protected characteristics under NICN Rules Order 14(2): age, gender, marital status, disability, religion, race, colour, nationality, family situation, union affiliation, health and ethnic origin. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- HIV & AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act 2014:
  - Employers with 5 or more employees must, within 12 calendar months of starting business and in consultation with employees, adopt a written HIV workplace policy consistent with the National HIV/AIDS Workplace Policy, and submit it to the Minister of Labour for approval.
  - Reported fines: ₦250,000 for the first year of default and ₦10,000 per day for failure to lodge.
  - The Act covers public and private employers and bars dismissal or coerced testing based on HIV status. — [BusinessDay, "HIV and AIDS anti-discrimination law and mandatory policy"](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/hiv-and-aids-anti-discrimination-law-and-mandatory-policy/); [Southern Africa Litigation Centre](https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/news-release-warning-against-hiv-discriminating-employers/); [NACA](https://naca.gov.ng/press-release-international-workers-day-2018/)
- Discrimination Against Persons with Disabilities (Prohibition) Act 2018:
  - It prohibits disability discrimination. Fines are ₦1,000,000 for corporate bodies, and ₦100,000 or 6 months' imprisonment for individuals.
  - The 5% employment quota in ss.28–29 is described as applying to employers in public organisations. One outlet describes it as also applying to corporate entities.
  - Private firms (MTN, Unilever, Access Bank and others) have voluntarily pledged 5%. — [ICIR, Six things about Disability Act](https://www.icirnigeria.org/six-key-things-you-must-know-about-disability-act-2018/); [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/8-things-to-know-about-nigerias-discrimination-against-persons-with-disabilities-act/); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/mtn-unilever-others-pledge-5-employment-opportunity-to-pwds/); [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/?p=56438)
- Lagos: the Lagos State Special People's Law 2011 prohibits disability discrimination in employment within Lagos. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)
- Lagos State Safety Commission:
  - It issues safety compliance certificates. Officials say these are free, and online registration takes under 5 minutes. — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/?p=1167774)
  - In 2022 the Commission proposed a ₦150,000 annual audit fee for tech hubs and co-working spaces (₦50k documentation + ₦100k certification), then suspended it after backlash. It said the fee targets facilities with public footfall. — [Gazette](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-directs-tech-hubs-co-working-spaces-to-pay-n150000-safety-fee/); [TechCabal, Sept 2022](https://techcabal.com/2022/09/28/lagos-safety-commission-suspends-tech-hub-audits/)
  - Elevator registration and certification for high-rises had a 31 March 2026 deadline. — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/safety-lagos-gives-high-rise-owners-18-days-to-register-elevators/)
- Whistleblowing: there is no comprehensive statute (the Whistle Blower Protection Bill 2019 is pending). Sector codes and the FOI Act s.27 provide limited protection. — [GLI 2025](https://isc.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GLIEMP25_Chapter-13_Nigeria.pdf)

### Inferences
- Minimum policy set for HomeAI:
  - an anti-harassment and anti-discrimination policy with a grievance procedure, extended to field agents in substance
  - an HIV/AIDS workplace policy lodged with the Federal Ministry of Labour within 12 months of commencement once there are 5 or more employees
  - a disability non-discrimination and reasonable-accommodation statement
  - an NDPA-compliant employee privacy notice
  - an occupational health and safety policy, especially for field agents doing repairs and cleaning (Factories Act and Lagos Safety Commission)
- The *Awogu* real-estate case is a relevant warning for a prop-tech workplace.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the HIV Act's employee threshold (5) or penalty amounts against the statute text. Some commentators cite other thresholds.
- I did not verify the Disability Act's accessibility transition period (5 years from 2019) or its private-sector obligations.
- The Lagos State Safety Commission Law 2011 duties for ordinary office employers (registration, safety officer, incident reporting) were not found.
- I found no Lagos-specific statute on employment terms, sexual harassment or leave for private employers.
- The Factories Act / OSH obligations for field work were not researched. The OSH Bill 2025 is pending.
