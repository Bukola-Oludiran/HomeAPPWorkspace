# Corporate Formation, Registration and Foreign-Ownership Requirements for HomeAI in Nigeria (as of October 2026)

Research date: 26 October 2026. Source quality legend used below: **[Primary]** = statute, gazette, government site or the government's own handbook; **[Law firm/Big 4]** = reputable professional commentary; **[Secondary]** = news, consultancy blog or registration agent. Many fee figures circulate only through agent blogs, and they often conflict. Where they do, the conflict is noted.

## 1. Entity options under CAMA 2020, minimum share capital, directors/shareholders, CAC online process, fees and timelines

### Takeaway
For HomeAI, the practical vehicle is a **private company limited by shares (Ltd)**. A business name gives no limited liability, cannot get a Startup Act label, and is unsuitable for escrow or payments. An LLP is possible but unusual for a VC-fundable tech platform. The statutory minimum issued share capital for a Nigerian-owned private company is **N100,000**. In practice, a company with any foreign shareholding is held to **N100 million**. CAC fees were re-gazetted with effect from **1 August 2025**: roughly **N10,000 per N1m of share capital** for private companies, plus name reservation, stamp duty and any agent fees. Incorporation typically takes **3–14 working days** if documents are clean.

### Cited Findings

**Entity types and structure**
- CAMA 2020 replaced "authorised share capital" with a **minimum issued share capital**. Under s.27(2) the floor is **N100,000 for private companies** (it was N10,000 under CAMA 1990) and **N2 million for public companies**. **[Law firm]** — [Mondaq (Part 5: Share Capital)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023802/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-5-share-capital); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/companies/article/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-5-share-capital/amp/)
- The figure is a floor, not a fixed amount: a company may set a higher figure but not a lower one. Unissued shares no longer count as share capital. Under the Companies Regulations 2021, existing companies had to issue their unallotted shares by a deadline extended to **31 December 2022**. **[Law firm/Secondary]** — [vLex/ThisDay analysis](https://ng.vlex.com/vid/cama-2020-practical-implications-925842726); [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2023/01/09/cama-2020-and-end-of-authorised-share-capital-regime-implications-for-companies/); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1061664/cama-2020-share-capital-requirements-under-nigerian-law)
- An increase in share capital takes effect only if 25% of the issued capital, including the increase, is paid up. **[Secondary]** — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/companies/article/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-5-share-capital/amp/)
- CAMA 2020 allows **single-member (single-shareholder) private companies**. A **small company may have a single director** and need not appoint a **company secretary**. **[Law firm]** — [Mondaq (Part 1: Small companies, single director and single member companies)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023656/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-1-small-companies-single-director-and-single-member-companies); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=459663)
- A private Ltd may have **1 to 50 shareholders**. A business name is held by **1–2 persons** (sole proprietor or simple partnership). PSC disclosure is required **at incorporation**. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults (updated March 2026)](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)
- **LLP (CAMA Part C):** at least **two partners** (ss.753(1)(a)–(b)). At least **two designated partners must be individuals, and at least one must be resident in Nigeria** (s.749). If all partners are corporate bodies, they may nominate individuals as designated partners. Designated partners are responsible for the LLP's CAMA compliance. **[Law firm]** — [LawPavilion (Atoyebi SAN)](https://blog.lawpavilion.com/an-overview-of-limited-liability-partnerships-under-cama-2020); [Mondaq (Part 2: Partnership structures)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1023658/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-2-partnership-structures)
- **Foreign-owned companies in practice:** one 2025 law-firm guide lists the CAC steps for a foreign-owned Ltd as: a Nigerian registered office, **at least two directors**, a company secretary, and issued share capital of at least N100m. **[Law firm]** — [Resolution Law Firm via Mondaq, 1 Sept 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide). *(This is practice and advice for foreign-owned companies. CAMA itself allows single-director small companies, and a foreign-owned company cannot be a "small company" anyway; see §2.)*
- Foreign nationals may be shareholders and directors. **Foreign directors must provide notarised, appropriately authenticated ID.** Diaspora Nigerians can register fully online. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)

**CAC online process**
- The steps are: (1) choose the entity type; (2) reserve the name on the CAC portal (pre.cac.gov.ng); (3) prepare the forms, the **MEMART** (for companies), the PSC disclosure, and director IDs and addresses; (4) submit and pay through Remita or card; (5) CAC review and any query; (6) the certificate is issued digitally. **Digital certificates are the original legal documents.** A reserved name is valid for **60 days**. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/); the 60-day validity is corroborated by [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)
- After incorporation the company may automatically receive a **TIN** from FIRS (now the Nigeria Revenue Service; see Gaps). If it does not, it must go to an FIRS office. Stamp duty on share capital is paid at filing. **[Law firm]** — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)
- **Timelines in practice:** business name 24–72 hours after payment; private Ltd **3–14 working days** with correct documents; company limited by guarantee 60–120 days (needs AGF approval); incorporated trustees 6–8 weeks. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)

**Official fees (current schedule)**
- New CAC service fees are in **Official Gazette No. 92 of 29 May 2025 (Statutory Instrument No. 6)**, **effective 1 August 2025**. **[Secondary reporting of a Primary gazette]** — [TheNigeriaLawyer, 20 June 2025](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - Name reservation: **N5,000** (all entity types), per TheNigeriaLawyer's reading of the gazette. *Conflict:* EBC Consults (March 2026) lists N1,000 for business-name and Ltd name reservation. Legit.ng gives N1,000–N5,000. — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/); [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/guides/1709408-everything-budget-cac-registration-fees-nigeria/)
  - Incorporation of a small company or other private company: **N10,000 per N1m of share capital**. Public company: **N20,000 per N1m**. Company limited by guarantee: **N40,000**. — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - Increase of share capital (up to N500m): private **N10,000 per N1m**; public N20,000 per N1m. — [same](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - Certified true copies: N5,000 each. Post-incorporation filings (excluding annual returns): small company N5,000; other private N10,000; LLP N10,000; business name N5,000. Business-name change of name N10,000. Same-day business-name filing add-on N10,000. — [same](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - *Conflict on older or tiered rates:* several agent sites still quote the pre-August-2025 schedule (N10,000 flat for capital up to N1m, N5,000 per N1m from N1m to N500m, N7,500 per N1m above N500m). These figures should be treated as **superseded**. — [Truehost](https://truehost.com.ng/cac-registration-fees/); [ICA](https://ica.ng/complete-breakdown-of-cost-for-registering-a-company-with-cac-in-nigeria-in-2024/); [Qrafteq](https://qrafteq.com/how-to-calculate-your-cac-fees-in-2025/)
  - **Business name registration fee:** EBC Consults gives N20,000 in its fee table but "about N11,000" in its FAQ, so the source is internally inconsistent. TheNigeriaLawyer's summary lists no separate figure. — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)
- **All-in budget estimates (agent, including stamp duty and professional fees):** business name N40k–N50k; private Ltd with up to N1m capital N50k–N150k; Ltd with N1m–N500m capital N80k–N250k+. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)
- A **CAC–SMEDAN programme** registered **250,000 micro and small businesses free** in September 2025. Further free windows were expected in 2026; this is unconfirmed. Such programmes have generally covered business names, not Ltd companies. — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)

### Inferences
- **HomeAI should be a private company limited by shares.** A business name has no separate legal personality, cannot hold a Startup Act label (s.13 requires a limited liability company), and would be a poor counterparty for escrow, payment and licensing.
- With all-Nigerian founders, HomeAI could incorporate at N1m issued capital for about N10,000 in CAC filing fees under the August 2025 schedule, plus stamp duty and name reservation. It may still need more capital for sector licences (for example CBN payment or escrow licences), which another research stream covers.
- With any foreign shareholder, the **N100m issued capital** expected by CAC and Interior (see §3) means roughly **N1,000,000 in CAC filing fees alone** at N10,000 per N1m. *This is my own arithmetic from the gazetted rate, not a quoted figure; verify against the portal fee calculator.* Stamp duty comes on top.

## 2. PSC / beneficial ownership, annual returns, filing deadlines, late-filing penalties, small-company exemptions

### Takeaway
Every company must disclose **natural-person PSCs at a 5% threshold** under CAMA s.119 and the **PSC Regulations 2022**. The disclosures go to a public register at bor.cac.gov.ng. Every company must also file **annual returns** under CAMA s.417. CAC enforces both aggressively: more than **400,000 companies were struck off in 2025**, and a **sixth batch of 100,000** received 90-day notices in **July 2026**. Late-filing penalties under the August 2025 schedule are small (a one-off N2,000 plus N200 a day for a non-small private company). The real risk is strike-off, which can only be reversed by petition to the Federal High Court. Small companies are exempt from audit, but **a company with any foreign shareholder cannot be a "small company."**

### Cited Findings

**PSC / beneficial ownership**
- CAC made the **Persons with Significant Control Regulations** on **23 November 2022** under CAMA s.119, with ministerial approval under s.867. **[Big 4/Law firm]** — [PwC Nigeria](https://pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/persons-with-significant-control-regulations-2022.pdf); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1299382/persons-with-significant-control-regulations-2022)
- A person is a PSC if they directly or indirectly hold **at least 5% of shares or interest**, or **at least 5% of voting rights**, or can appoint or remove a majority of directors, or exercise "significant influence or control." That last term covers the ability to direct or materially influence finances, management, operations or structure, or to derive significant economic benefit. PSCs must be **natural persons**, and corporate intermediaries are looked through. — [PwC Nigeria](https://pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/persons-with-significant-control-regulations-2022.pdf); [ÆLEX](https://aelex.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/THE-BENEFICIAL-OWNERSHIP-REGISTER-IMPLEMENTING-THE-FINANCIAL-ACTION-TASK-FORCES-RECOMMENDATIONS-ON-MONEY-LAUNDERING-AND-TERRORISM-FINANCING-IN-NIGERIA-1.pdf)
  - *Conflict:* EBC Consults (2026) describes a **25%** PSC test. This contradicts the 2022 Regulations as summarised by PwC, so treat **5% as authoritative**. — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)
- CAC must keep a public PSC register, accessible online at **bor.cac.gov.ng** (CAMA s.119(3)). PwC criticises the "significant influence" wording as wide enough to catch executives and lenders. — [PwC Nigeria](https://pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/persons-with-significant-control-regulations-2022.pdf)
- In **September 2026** the Registrar-General, Hussaini Magaji, called for **harmonising Nigeria's beneficial-ownership registers** under a "coordinated national framework." CSOs pressed for interoperability with procurement, property and financial databases. This is a policy direction, not yet law. **[Secondary/news]** — [Peoples Gazette/NAN, 15 Sept 2026](https://gazettengr.com/cac-seeks-harmonisation-of-beneficial-ownership-registers-for-increased-transparency/)
- Magaji was appointed Registrar-General on **13 October 2023**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussaini_Ishaq_Magaji)

**Annual returns and deadlines**
- Annual returns are required by **CAMA s.417**, and PSC disclosure by **s.119**. Strike-off powers are in **s.692(3)–(4)**. **[Law firm]** — [Olaniwun Ajayi LP, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.olaniwunajayi.net/blog/cacs-renewed-strike-off-campaign-ensuring-corporate-compliance-in-nigeria/)
- Filing timing in practice: companies file **within 42 days of each AGM**; business names file yearly from the registration anniversary. **[Secondary]** — [EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-registration-nigeria/)
- **Annual return filing fees (August 2025 schedule):** small company **N5,000**; company limited by guarantee N10,000. Private companies other than small: **N100,000** (capital above N100m up to N500m), **N150,000** (above N500m up to N1bn), **N200,000** (above N1bn). — [TheNigeriaLawyer (gazette reproduction)](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - *Note:* the reproduced table marks "NA" for private non-small companies in the N100,000–N100m band, which leaves the fee for a non-small private company with capital at or below N100m unclear. A foreign-owned HomeAI at exactly N100m would sit at this boundary. — [same](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
- **Late annual return penalties (August 2025 schedule), shown as one-off + daily:** small company N1,000 + N100/day; private (other than small) **N2,000 + N200/day**; public N5,000 + N500/day; LLP N2,000 + N200/day; business name N5,000 + N100/day. Penalties apply to **both the entity and its officers**. **Other non-compliance penalties:** small company N5,000 + N100/day; private N10,000 + N200/day; LLP N10,000 + N200/day. — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/full-list-fg-releases-official-gazette-on-new-cac-service-fees-effective-august-1/)
  - *Conflict:* agent sites give different figures. One quotes N5,000 or N10,000 per year plus N1,000 per officer. One claims daily penalties are "currently suspended," which is unconfirmed. Another claims PSC default penalties of N5,000–N50,000 a day, which is also unconfirmed. — [cacregister.com.ng](https://cacregister.com.ng/blog/penalty-for-late-filing-of-cac-annual-returns-2026-update); [Docufy calculator](https://docufy.ng/tools/cac-annual-return-penalty-calculator); [Qrafteq](https://qrafteq.com/cac-penalties-nigeria-2025-guide/)

**Strike-off enforcement (2024–2026)**
- On **30 December 2024**, CAC published a list of **100,000** defaulting companies, some dormant for more than ten years. After a 90-day window it delisted companies under s.692(3)–(4). A **28 July 2025** notice opened another 90-day window covering annual returns and PSC filings. — [Olaniwun Ajayi LP](https://www.olaniwunajayi.net/blog/cacs-renewed-strike-off-campaign-ensuring-corporate-compliance-in-nigeria/); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/national/cac-to-strike-off-non-compliant-companies-from-official-register/)
- In February 2026, CAC said it **struck off more than 400,000 companies in 2025**. In **July 2026** it began a **sixth batch of 100,000 companies**, giving them **90 days** to file outstanding annual returns and PSC/beneficial-ownership information. Companies that miss the deadline will be "struck off the Register without further notice." **[News]** — [Premium Times, 15 July 2026](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/business/business-news/895765-cac-begins-sixth-batch-of-company-strike-off-targets-100000-companies.html); [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/cac-begins-sixth-batch-of-company-strike-off-targets-100000-firms/); [Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/cac-opens-sixth-deregistration-round-giving-100000-nigerian-companies-90-days-to-comply/)
- A struck-off company cannot carry on business. Restoration requires a **petition to the Federal High Court**, which is costly and slow. — [Olaniwun Ajayi LP](https://www.olaniwunajayi.net/blog/cacs-renewed-strike-off-campaign-ensuring-corporate-compliance-in-nigeria/)

**Small company regime and audit exemption**
- **CAMA s.394 "small company":** a private company limited by shares with **turnover not above N120m**, **net assets not above N60m**, **no foreign shareholders**, no government ownership, and **directors holding at least 51%** of equity. **[Law firm]** — [Mondaq (Part 1)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023656/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-1-small-companies-single-director-and-single-member-companies)
- **CAMA s.402:** a company is exempt from audit for a financial year if it has not carried on business since incorporation or is a small company under s.394. Insurance companies, banks and other companies CAC prescribes are excluded. Small companies do not have to file audited financial statements every year. — [LawGlobalHub (s.402 text)](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/?p=59057); [Mondaq (Part 1)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023656/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-what-you-need-to-know-part-1-small-companies-single-director-and-single-member-companies); [Tekedia](https://www.tekedia.com/audits-under-the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)

**New disclosure rule on business letters (2026)**
- A CAC public notice of **7 July 2026** requires every company to show, in clear and legible characters on business letters and official correspondence: every director's forename (or initials) and surname, any former names, the **nationality of each non-Nigerian director**, the registered name, the RC number and the registered office address. The legal basis is CAMA **ss.304(1), 304(2) and 729(1)(c)**. **Full enforcement began 1 August 2026**, "with attendant sanctions." Business names and incorporated trustees are excluded. — [Legit.ng, 11 July 2026](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/industry/1718745-cac-companies-fresh-deadline-business-letter-disclosure-rules/); [Daily Nigerian](https://dailynigerian.com/business-names-ngos-exempted-from-cacs-new-letterhead-directive-magaji/)

### Inferences
- If HomeAI has **any foreign shareholder, it is not a small company**. It therefore loses the audit exemption and the single-director and no-secretary concessions, and pays the higher annual-return fee bands. A wholly Nigerian-owned HomeAI could use those concessions until turnover passes N120m.
- A founder's offshore holding company (for example a Delaware or UK parent used for fundraising) does not avoid PSC disclosure. The 5% look-through to natural persons means every founder or investor at 5% or more must be disclosed.
- HomeAI's letterheads, invoices and probably its email footers and website (by analogy; not confirmed) should carry the s.304 particulars from launch.
- The pattern of 2024–2026 enforcement suggests CAC now treats non-filing as grounds for removal rather than as a matter for fines. A calendar-driven company-secretarial process is the main control.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact statutory deadlines from the CAMA text: the first annual return about 18 months after incorporation, the first AGM within 18 months, and whether small or single-member companies may dispense with AGMs. These are commonly stated but unverified here.
- I found no official penalty figure for **PSC non-disclosure** specifically, nor confirmation of any PSC-change filing window (practitioners often cite 7 or 14 days).
- I found no confirmation of whether **PSC Regulations 2022 have been amended** since 2022.

## 3. Foreign ownership: minimum capital, NIPC, Business Permit, expatriate quota, CERPAC, CCI, sector restrictions

### Takeaway
Nigeria permits **100% foreign ownership** in most sectors, including tech and real-estate listings. The sequence for a foreign-owned company is: **CAC (with N100m issued capital in practice) → open a bank account → bring in capital and obtain a CCI → register with NIPC → obtain a Business Permit from the Ministry of Interior (N100m paid-up) → apply for expatriate quota and CERPAC for any foreign staff**. Since **May 2025**, all Interior and Immigration steps run through a new digital **Expatriate Administration System (EAS)** with sharply higher fees. The **CCI** is what makes the statutory guarantee of profit and capital repatriation usable in practice. Real-estate agency activity is a **DNFBP** that must register with **SCUML before commencing business**.

### Cited Findings

**Minimum share capital for foreign participation**
- The Ministry of Interior's **Handbook on Expatriate Quota Administration (Revised 2022)** issues Business Permits only to wholly foreign-owned or JV companies, and sets **N100,000,000 minimum paid-up capital**. **Imported equipment or machinery may count toward paid-up capital.** **[Primary]** — [Ministry of Interior C&B Handbook (hosted May 2025)](https://candb.interior.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/CB-Business-HANDBOOK.pdf)
- **CAC position, which is practice rather than published rule:** CAC announced a N100m requirement in **December 2023** and withdrew the announcement three days later. Techpoint says CAC "has not formally updated its rules" but has been "strictly implementing" N100m **since July 2024**. Filings at N10m may be queried. CAC leans toward **issued** capital, while Interior requires **paid-up** capital. Techpoint also says the rule applies to companies with foreign **shareholders or directors** regardless of percentage. **[Secondary]** — [Techpoint Africa, 15 Apr 2025](https://techpoint.africa/guide/share-capital-foreign-companies-nigeria/)
- Law-firm guides consistently state N100m for foreign-owned companies. Resolution Law Firm says N100m issued at CAC and evidence of N100m capital importation or availability for the Business Permit. **[Law firm]** — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq, 1 Sept 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/corporate-commercial-law/all-you-need-to-know-about-registering-a-foreign-owned-business-in-nigeria)
- *Unverified claim:* one forms site cites "USD 200,000 minimum capital" under NIPC Act s.18 and sector exclusions under s.31. These figures are not corroborated, and I believe NIPC Act s.18 is about registration, not a capital floor. Treat them as **unreliable**. — [legalclarity.org](https://legalclarity.org/nigerian-investment-promotion-commission-act-explained/)

**NIPC registration**
- Sources disagree on whether NIPC registration is **mandatory**. Resolution Law Firm (2025) says "any company with foreign shareholders must register with the NIPC after incorporation." A Mondaq/Legal500 piece ties this to **NIPC Act s.19** (mandatory before commencing business). Another source calls it "recommended, not compulsory." Registration starts with **NIPC Form 1** plus a non-refundable fee, and produces a **Business Registration Certificate**. It is a prerequisite for incentives such as **Pioneer Status**. — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide); [Mondaq/Legal500](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1347292/all-you-need-to-know-about-registering-a-foreign-owned-business-in-nigeria); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/how-a-foreigner-can-register-a-local-company-in-nigeria)
- **Sector restrictions:** NIPC applies a short "**negative list**" (arms, ammunition, narcotics and similar). Most sectors allow 100% foreign ownership. A Nigerian partner is required only in oil and gas, mining and gaming. — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)

**Certificate of Capital Importation (CCI) and repatriation**
- **NIPC Act s.24** guarantees a foreign investor "unconditional transferability of funds through any authorised dealer in freely convertible currency" of dividends and profits (net of taxes), loan servicing, and proceeds of sale or liquidation. The **Foreign Exchange (Monitoring and Miscellaneous Provisions) Act s.15** contains a parallel guarantee. — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://adeolaoyinlade.com/en/?p=1615); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/~/media/hogan-lovells/pdf/publication/foreign-investments-in-nigeriapreliminiary-legal-issues_pdf.pdf)
- The **CCI** is issued by the **authorised-dealer bank** that receives the inflow. Without a CCI, the s.24 guarantees are "effectively unenforceable in practice," and foreign exchange cannot be bought from the official market to repatriate proceeds. — [Adeola Oyinlade & Co](https://adeolaoyinlade.com/en/?p=1615); [Nigeria High Commission UK, "Foreign Investment Requirements and Protections"](https://www.nigeriahc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/FOREIGN-INVESTMENT-REQUIREMENTS-AND-PROTECTIONS.doc)
- The usual flow: incorporate, open a Nigerian corporate account (often a domiciliary account), remit capital from abroad, and the bank issues the CCI. — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/guide/share-capital-foreign-companies-nigeria/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/?p=44810)

**Business Permit (Ministry of Interior)**
- A Business Permit is required **before a foreign-owned company begins operations**. The application needs CAC documents, a business plan, evidence of office premises, evidence of at least N100m in capital importation or availability, and a **tax clearance certificate**. Resolution Law Firm says it is permanent and does not need renewal. **[Law firm]** — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide); [Ministry of Interior Handbook](https://candb.interior.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/CB-Business-HANDBOOK.pdf); [Mercator](https://www.mercator.net/our-thinking/latest-news/nigeria-application-and-amendment-of-business-permit-for-companies-with-foreign-participation/)

**2025 Expatriate Administration System (EAS): EQ, Business Permit and CERPAC go digital**
- From **1 May 2025**, the Ministry of Interior and the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS) require **expatriate quota, Business Permit and CERPAC applications to be made fully online**. The old **e-citibiz** portal was replaced by a new **Citizenship and Business (C&B) Management** platform. A "Legacy Records" feature syncs valid EQ approvals with e-CERPAC. **[Law firm/Big 4]** — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/nigeria-new-digital-process-announced-for-quota-business-permit-and-cerpac-applications.html); [EY Global Immigration Alert, Sept 2025](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/technical/tax-alerts/documents/nigeria-launches-new-online-platform-to-streamline-immigration-processes.pdf); [KPMG Nigeria via Mondaq, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/work-visas/1764986/modernising-mobility-assessing-nigerias-2025-immigration-reforms-and-the-road-ahead)
- **Expatriate quota:** slot lifespans are now **4–10 years** depending on EQ type and line of business. "Permanent Until Reviewed" slots are **capped at 10 years**. Statutory fees are paid **upfront and are non-refundable** if the application is rejected. **EQ fee increases from May 2025 range from 35% to over 500%.** EQ holders must train Nigerian understudies and file periodic returns. — [KPMG/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/work-visas/1764986/modernising-mobility-assessing-nigerias-2025-immigration-reforms-and-the-road-ahead); [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)
- **e-CERPAC** (Electronic Comprehensive Expatriate Residence Permit and Automated Card): manual applications have ended, and Polaris Bank no longer sells forms. The **US$2,000** fee is paid electronically. Long-term permits add an **Expatriate Comprehensive Insurance** fee of **US$1,000**. Expatriates enter on a **Subject-to-Regularisation (STR)** visa. — [KPMG/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/work-visas/1764986/modernising-mobility-assessing-nigerias-2025-immigration-reforms-and-the-road-ahead); [Legal500 (NIS e-CERPAC)](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/immigration/regulatory-insights-nis-launches-new-online-cerpac-platform); [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)
- **e-TWP** (short-term temporary work permits of up to 3 months): applied for online, with a **US$200** fee and a **US$400** insurance premium. **Visa on Arrival ended 1 May 2025** and was replaced by **e-Visa** (13 short-visit classes, decision about 48 hours, not extendable). **Overstay penalties of US$15 per day plus entry bans have applied since 1 September 2025.** — [KPMG/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/work-visas/1764986/modernising-mobility-assessing-nigerias-2025-immigration-reforms-and-the-road-ahead)
- A **foreign director who will work in Nigeria needs an EQ slot** and CERPAC. — [Resolution Law Firm/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1672318/setting-up-a-company-in-nigeria-as-a-foreigner-a-brief-guide)
- **Expatriate Employment Levy (EEL):** introduced 27 Feb 2024 at about **US$15,000 per expatriate director and US$10,000 per other expatriate**. **Implementation was suspended on 8 March 2024** after objections from the organised private sector. I found **no source showing it was revived through 2026**, and none of the 2025–2026 KPMG/EY/Fragomen alerts mentions it as live. ### Inferences
- **Nigerian founders only:** none of the NIPC, Business Permit, EQ or CCI steps apply. - The EAS reforms mean expatriate-hire budgets must allow for higher, non-refundable, upfront EQ fees and per-head CERPAC and insurance costs, roughly US$3,000 per long-term expatriate before EQ fees.

## 4. Nigeria Startup Act 2022: labelling, eligibility, benefits, implementation status

### Takeaway
The Act (signed **19 October 2022**) lets a qualifying **CAMA limited company under 10 years old**, whose objects are digital technology innovation and which has **at least one-third local shareholding held by Nigerian founders or co-founders**, apply through **startup.gov.ng** for a **"startup label" valid for 10 years**. The label opens access to more than 31 incentives in six categories. As of **August 2026**, the government says the portal, faster labelling timelines and a Seed Fund framework are in place. The **regulatory sandbox is still not operational**, and states are only being engaged. Implementation is partial. An HomeAI with no Nigerian founder shareholders would not qualify.

### Cited Findings
- **Primary text:** Nigeria Startup Act, 2022 (Act No. 32), published at startup.gov.ng. **[Primary]** — [Nigeria Startup Act 2022 (official PDF)](https://startup.gov.ng/doc/NIGERIA_STARTUP_ACT_2022_Final_Publication.pdf)
- **Eligibility (s.13):** registered as a limited liability company under CAMA; in existence **no more than 10 years** from incorporation; objects in digital technology innovation, with a digital technology product or process. Commentators also describe a requirement for **at least one-third local shareholding held by one or more Nigerian founders or co-founders**, though sources disagree on the subsection. — [PwC Nigeria, Nov 2022](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/nigeria-startup-act-positioning-the-country-for-the-fourth-revolution.pdf); [Royal Heritage Law](https://royalheritagelaw.com/the-nigeria-startup-act-2022/); [startup.africa](https://www.startup.africa/nigerias-startup-act-a-2025-guide-for-startups-and-investors/)
- **Pre-label:** a sole proprietorship or partnership can get a **6-month pre-label** status to allow it to convert to a limited company. **Label validity: 10 years** from issue. — [Vernia Legal](https://vernialegal.com.ng/the-nigeria-startup-act-2022-highlights-and-benefits/); [LawCare Nigeria](https://lawcarenigeria.com/nigeria-startup-act-2022/)
- **Benefits:** PwC says labelled startups may enjoy an **income-tax holiday of up to 5 years under Pioneer Status**. Other commentators read this more narrowly, as **expedited NIPC approval** of Pioneer Status for startups in PSI-eligible industries rather than an automatic holiday. — [PwC Nigeria](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/nigeria-startup-act-positioning-the-country-for-the-fourth-revolution.pdf); [Vernia Legal](https://vernialegal.com.ng/the-nigeria-startup-act-2022-highlights-and-benefits/)
- **Portal:** the **Startup Support and Engagement Portal (startup.gov.ng)** is the channel for label applications, business registration, access to government tools, applications for permits, licences and incentives, and complaints. It also labels VCs, hubs and innovation centres. **[Law firm]** — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/insights/establishment-of-the-startup-support-and-engagement-portal-pursuant-to-the-nigeria-startup-act/); [Acelera Law](https://www.acelera.law/publications/nigeria-startup-act-implementation-establishment-of-the-nigeria-startup-support-and-engaement-portal)
- **Implementation status (13 August 2026):** at the NSA Incentives Activation Co-Creation Session in Abuja, run by **ONDI** (a NITDA subsidiary), officials said the Act holds **more than 31 incentives in six categories** (tax/fiscal, regulatory support, funding access, exports/trade, ecosystem development, training). Delivery needs **more than 15 MDAs**. Already in place: the portal, **"improved startup labelling timelines"** and the **Startup Investment Seed Fund framework**. Still in progress: operationalising the **regulatory sandbox** and engaging **states** to adopt the Act. ONDI's Elma Andah: "No single institution can deliver all these incentives alone." No count of labelled startups was given. **[News]** — [BusinessDay, 13 Aug 2026](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/fg-moves-to-turn-nigerias-startup-act-into-funding-tax-and-export-gains-for-founders/)
- A late-2024 law-firm review ("Two Years On") assesses the Act's early implementation. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1699582/startup-act-in-nigeria-two-years-on)

### Inferences
- HomeAI fits the "digital technology innovation" objects test (an AI assistant and a proptech platform). The **one-third Nigerian-founder shareholding** is the binding constraint in the foreign-founder scenario. A purely foreign-founded HomeAI cannot be labelled unless Nigerian co-founders hold at least 33.3%.
- The label is worth getting for access to the portal, the Seed Fund and regulatory facilitation. Founders should **not count on the tax holiday** until it is confirmed under the new 2025 tax laws, since incentives are being "activated" rather than delivered automatically.

- I could not confirm whether the **Nigeria Tax Act 2025** (effective 2026) changed or preserved Pioneer Status and Startup Act tax incentives. This is important; the tax stream should cover it.

## 5. Intellectual property basics: trademarks, .ng domain, protecting "HomeAI"

### Takeaway
Register "HomeAI" (word mark and logo) at the **Trademarks Registry** through the **IPO Nigeria portal (iponigeria.fmiti.gov.ng)**, under the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment. File **one application per class**, likely Class 36 (real estate services), 42 (software/SaaS/AI) and 9 (downloadable apps). Registration lasts **7 years from filing**, then renews in **14-year** periods. **Nigeria does not allow Paris Convention priority claims** for trademarks, so foreign marks must be filed locally early. CAC name reservation **does not** confer trademark rights. Register the **.ng domain** (homeai.ng and homeai.com.ng) through a NiRA-accredited registrar.

### Cited Findings
- The **IPO Nigeria portal** handles filing, renewals and management of trademark, patent and design rights, and publishes a services and fees page. **[Primary]** — [IPO Nigeria – Services & Fees](https://iponigeria.fmiti.gov.ng/services-fees/); [IPO Nigeria home](https://iponigeria.fmiti.gov.ng/); [Portal guides](https://iponigeria.fmiti.gov.ng/resources/portal-guides/)
- **Process:** a separate application is needed for each class, and documents must be in English. **Conventional priority cannot be claimed** in Nigeria. After acceptance and publication, third parties have **2 months to oppose**. The grant fee is paid on the Notice of Allowance. **Term: 7 years from filing**, renewable for **14-year** periods. Renewal must be filed 3 months before expiry, with a surcharge for late renewal within one month. **[Secondary (IP cost guide), undated]** — [IP-Coster](https://ip-coster.com/IPGuides/trademark-nigeria)
- In **October 2025** the Registry issued a **new online Trademarks Journal**. — [IP Africa newsletter (Spoor & Fisher-type newsletter), Issue 13](https://a.storyblok.com/f/302089/x/6ef834b3a8/issue13.pdf)
- A dated comparative guide gave official costs of about US$41.66 for a single-class filing and US$4.16 for a search. **This is outdated; do not rely on it.** — [Mondaq Trademarks Comparative Guide](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/intellectual-property/788900/trademarks-comparative-guide)
- **.ng domains:** NiRA reported **241,000 active .ng domains** (98,285 new registrations and 71,470 renewals in the past year) in **June 2026**. It plans wholesale subsidies through registrars to lower prices. — [Nairametrics, 8 June 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/08/nira-records-241000-active-ng-domains-targets-nationwide-adoption/); [Guardian (NiRA price slash)](https://guardian.ng/technology/tech/council-tasks-nira-on-price-slash-body-registers-1-542-ng-domain-names/)

### Inferences
- "HomeAI" is a fairly descriptive compound ("home" + "AI"). The Registry may object on distinctiveness grounds, or treat it as Part B rather than Part A under the Trade Marks Act. A stylised logo mark plus a word mark gives better protection. A clearance search before brand spend is strongly advised.
- The company name (CAC), the trademark (Registry) and the domain (NiRA) are three separate systems. Securing all three early, plus .com and social handles, is the standard defensive set.

- I did not verify any **Trade Marks Act reform** (a long-pending IP Commission bill) for 2025–2026.
- I found no current **.ng retail price** or NiRA eligibility rules (for example, whether .com.ng needs a CAC number).

## 6. State and local permits: Lagos State and FCT (Abuja)

The process has two stages: a pre-scrutiny assessment of the proposed sign on the premises, then registration and permit. LASAA should respond within about 4 weeks. **[Secondary (LSETF page)]** — [LSETF](https://www.lsetf.ng/node/151)
