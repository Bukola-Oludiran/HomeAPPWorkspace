# Real-Estate Sector Regulation and AML Rules for a Nigerian Property Listing, Verification and Transaction Platform (HomeAI), as of October 2026

Research date: 26 October 2026. These notes rely on the gazetted SCUML DNFBP Regulations 2024 (primary text), the MLPPA 2022 text reproduced by LawPadi, the ESVARBON Act text on PLAC's Laws of Nigeria, a Templars law-firm review of the LASRERA Law (which has section references), and news coverage from 2025–2026. Where a source is a blog or vendor site, it is flagged.

## 1. LASRERA and the Lagos State Real Estate Regulatory Authority Law (signed 7 Feb 2022, styled "2021")

### Takeaway
Anyone in Lagos who deals in real estate as an agent, realtor, developer, facility manager or property manager must register with LASRERA and hold an annual permit. The Law requires a separate client account, transaction records and remittance of rent within 7 working days. It caps agency fees at 10% for lettings and 15% where several permit holders share a sale or lease mandate. The state said in June 2025 that dealing with unregistered practitioners is also an offence. That matters directly for a marketplace that hosts agents. The Law does not expressly mention online or proptech platforms.

### Cited Findings
**Status and naming**
- The Law was signed by Governor Sanwo-Olu on 7 February 2022 and is styled the "Lagos State Real Estate Regulatory Authority Law, 2021". It establishes LASRERA (s.2(1)) to regulate real estate transactions, reduce fraud, adopt a code of ethics and settle disputes by mediation — [Templars review](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- It repealed the Lagos State Estate Agency Regulatory Authority Law Ch. L28, Laws of Lagos State 2015. Sources disagree on whether to call it the 2021 or 2022 Law — [search summary citing JEE / Gelias reviews](https://oldsite.jee.africa/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Lagos-State-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-2021.pdf); [Gelias review](https://www.gelias.com/images/Newsletter/A_Review_of_the_Lagos_State_Real_Estate_Regulatory_Law_2021_Apr_2022.pdf)

**Functions (s.6)** — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Keeps a register of permits held by real estate service professionals.
- Sets up monitoring teams and inspections to enforce the Lagos Tenancy Law.
- Receives and investigates complaints, and collates data on property transactions.
- Registers tenancy transactions and agreements longer than 5 years.
- Confirms that fees and taxes on real estate transactions are paid.
- Registers and issues permits to persons and organisations dealing in real estate, and sets permit renewal conditions.
- The Governing Board assesses and fixes "reasonable regulatory fees".

**Who must register, and the conditions**
- Individuals (s.26(2)) must:
  - be Nigerian, or hold a valid work permit;
  - be at least 18 years old and hold a LASRRA (Lagos residents registration) number;
  - have an ascertainable office in Lagos;
  - hold at least WASC, GCE or NECO;
  - keep proper records of transactions and operate a separate client account;
  - have 3 years' Tax Clearance Certificates;
  - have at least a business name registered with the CAC.
  — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Companies (s.26(3)) must:
  - be CAC-registered;
  - keep proper records of transactions and operate a separate client account;
  - have at least one director who meets the individual conditions;
  - have work permits for all non-Nigerian directors;
  - have 3 years' Tax Clearance Certificates.
  — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Property developers, facility managers and property management companies, whether individuals or organisations, must register and declare their current projects before they get permits (s.27) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Foreigners or foreign companies investing in real estate need the Governor's permission through LASRERA. Land investment by foreigners is limited to 25 years, including any renewal option (s.28) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- A permit lasts at most 1 year and is renewable. A fee is payable on issue (s.29). Permits lapse on death, incapacity, bankruptcy or liquidation (s.30) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

**Fees**
- The annual registration fee is reported as ₦1 million for agents and developers. Stakeholders have asked LASRERA to reduce it. This is press reporting, not an official fee schedule — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1664245-stakeholders-urge-lasrera-review-registration-fees-n1-million/)

**Operational standards (s.32(1))** — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- (a) Developers must ensure buildings are fit for habitation and register the Lagos State Building Control Agency (LASBCA) Certificate of Completion.
- (b) Agents:
  - must not represent two principals on the same transaction;
  - must not collect money from more than one prospective client for the same premises;
  - must remit tenancy money to the landlord within 7 working days, unless the landlord directs otherwise in writing, and must receipt it.
- (c) Developers must give the buyer physical possession within 7 working days of payment, or once the contract's conditions are met.
- (d) A practitioner must declare any personal interest in a property.
- (e) A practitioner must ensure clients meet government obligations such as Withholding Tax and VAT.
- (f) A practitioner must not prepare legal documents. Every tenancy agreement or contract must be prepared and sealed by a Legal Practitioner, on the Legal Practitioners' scale of fees.
- (g) An agent must get the principal's consent before collecting money from a prospective client.
- (h) A practitioner must refund rent or money within 7 days if possession is not delivered.
- (i) Late refunds attract interest at the prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) rate.

**Fee caps (s.32(2)–(4))** — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Letting or lease: no more than 10% of the total rent collected.
- Sale or purchase where two or more permit holders are retained: no more than 15% of total proceeds.
- Developers must price at fair market value as determined by an estate surveyor and valuer.
- Off-plan contracts must include a clause indemnifying the client if the project is not completed or the contract is breached.
- The June 2025 government statement put agency fees at "0 to 10 percent". It also said practitioners may not demand more than one year's rent in advance, or collect payment without delivering the service — [Nairametrics, 28 Jun 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/28/lagos-govt-declares-real-estate-practice-without-lasrera-registration-illegal/)

**Escrow and client funds**
- The Law requires a "separate client account" as a registration condition (s.26). It also requires 7-day remittance and refund rules (s.32). I found no provision expressly creating an escrow regime — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

**Sanctions**
- Failure to register (s.48(a)): a fine of at least ₦250,000 for an individual, or at least ₦1,000,000 for an organisation — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- General non-compliance — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf):

  | Offender | Sanction |
  |---|---|
  | Registered individual | Permit revocation, or ₦100,000 plus ₦10,000 per day |
  | Unregistered individual | ₦100,000 plus ₦25,000 per day |
  | Registered organisation | Revocation, or ₦500,000 plus ₦30,000 per day |
  | Unregistered organisation | ₦500,000 plus ₦50,000 per day |

- The Law removed imprisonment as a punishment for individuals — [search summary of Gelias/JEE reviews](https://www.gelias.com/images/Newsletter/A_Review_of_the_Lagos_State_Real_Estate_Regulatory_Law_2021_Apr_2022.pdf)

**Disputes**
- A Committee of Inquiry hears complaints (s.33). It can recommend suspending or revoking a permit, and appeals go to court (s.34) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Mediation begins by petition (s.35). Criminal allegations such as fraud or obtaining by false pretence go to the Police and the Directorate of Public Prosecutions (s.35(4)–(5)) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- A mediated memorandum of understanding (MOU), once endorsed by a Magistrate or Judge, is enforceable as a court judgment (s.36) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

**Enforcement, 2025–2026**
- On 28 June 2025, Barr. Barakat Odunuga-Bakare, Special Adviser on Housing and head of LASRERA, announced that it is "an offence to practice real estate business" without LASRERA registration. This applies to commission agents, realtors and practitioners. She also said it is an offence to deal with unregistered persons or organisations. The public was told to verify registration and report overcharging. The article gave no enforcement statistics — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/28/lagos-govt-declares-real-estate-practice-without-lasrera-registration-illegal/); [Housing TV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/lagos-real-estate-regulation/)
- One 2026 guide claims LASRERA recovered over ₦270 million from unregistered operators. This is unverified: the source is a blog and I found no primary source — [2WV Digital](https://2wvdigital.com.ng/how-to-start-a-real-estate-business-in-nigeria/)
- In August 2025, Lagos labelled 176 estate developments in Eti-Osa, Ajah, Ibeju-Lekki and other areas illegal for lacking layout approvals. Sealing of structures continued into 2026. This was planning enforcement, not LASRERA — [Nigeria Housing Market newsletter](https://www.newsletter.nigeriahousingmarket.com/p/lagos-seals-estates-without-approved-layout-plans-in-renewed-enforcement-crackdown); [AllAfrica, Sep 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509110074.html)

### Inferences
- HomeAI may itself need a LASRERA permit as a corporate practitioner in two cases: if it collects or holds rent or deposits, or if it earns commission on lets or sales. The Law covers persons or organisations "dealing in real estate transactions", and the fee caps and remittance rules are framed around money collected.
- A pure listing or advertising portal that never touches funds or earns commission is less clearly covered. The June 2025 statement that "dealing with" unregistered practitioners is an offence suggests HomeAI should require and check LASRERA registration numbers before letting Lagos agents or developers list.
- HomeAI's rent payment flows should mirror s.32:
  - pay landlords within 7 working days;
  - issue receipts;
  - refund automatically within 7 days if possession fails, with interest at the CBN rate if late;
  - take money from only one applicant per unit;
  - obtain the landlord's consent before collecting.
- Under s.32(1)(f), HomeAI must not let agents generate lease documents themselves. In-app lease templates or e-signed leases should be prepared and sealed by a legal practitioner.

### Gaps
- I found no official, current LASRERA fee schedule or registration categories by tier. The LASRERA portal was not retrieved. The ₦1m figure comes from press reports.
- I found no confirmed prosecution or sanction of an online platform by LASRERA, and no LASRERA guidance on proptech platforms.
- I could not verify the full statutory text. Section numbers come from the Templars review.

## 2. FCT (Abuja) and other states

### Takeaway
The FCT has no real-estate regulatory authority or statutory agency-fee cap equivalent to LASRERA. Abuja fees follow market custom of roughly 5–10%. Federal bills to cap advance rent or create a rent framework for the FCT have been proposed, but none has been found to have passed.

### Cited Findings
- No enacted FCT rule caps estate agent fees. Abuja's custom is about 10% of annual rent, with reports of agents demanding 20% in high-demand areas such as Wuse 2. These are blog and market sources — [Ownkey blog](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/real-estate-agent-commission-fees-nigeria-who-pays); [Housing TV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/abuja-tenants-groan-rent-agency-fees/)
- A "FCT Advance Rent Regulation and Tenants' Protection Bill, 2026 (HB. 2839)" is reported to propose standard tenancy agreements, an advance-rent cap and fines of up to ₦5m for landlords and agents. Its status and bill number are unverified and I found no evidence it has passed — [search-result summary of Ownkey/Streamline Feed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/nigerian-lawmakers-propose-fct-rent-authority-to-curb-arbitrary-abuja-hikes)
- An earlier Senate bill sponsored by Sen. Smart Adeyemi would have cut advance rent for new Abuja tenants to 3 months and moved the FCT toward monthly rent. It passed second reading. This is older, and I found no evidence it was enacted — [TheCable](https://thecable.ng/bill-to-compel-abuja-landlords-to-receive-rent-monthly-passes-second-reading-at-senate); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/features/law/pros-cons-of-the-proposed-federal-rent-legislation/)
- A NILDS paper notes there is no comprehensive national framework for fair rent, and that reform is happening state by state, including the Lagos 2025 Bill — [NILDS repository](https://ir.nilds.gov.ng/handle/123456789/3466)
- The FCTA is enforcing ground rent and land use fee compliance in Abuja. This affects due diligence on title — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/fcta-commences-enforcement-of-ground-rent-and-land-use-fee-collections)
- The FCCPC is the most active enforcer against real estate firms in Abuja (see section 8).

### Inferences
- In the FCT, HomeAI's obligations are federal: the MLPPA and SCUML, the FCCPC Act, the ESVARBON Act, and the Land Use Act as administered by the FCTA and AGIS. There is no state real estate permit equivalent to LASRERA.
- Landlord-tenant recovery in the FCT is generally understood to run under the Recovery of Premises Act (Cap 544 LFN (Abuja) 1990). I did not verify this from a primary source (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I found no verified text or citation of the FCT Recovery of Premises Act's notice periods, or of any FCTA rule on agents.
- I could not confirm HB 2839's status from the National Assembly tracker.
- Other states such as Ogun, Rivers and Oyo were not researched in detail. I found no source on their real-estate regulators within budget.

## 3. ESVARBON / NIESV: reserved activities for registered estate surveyors and valuers

### Takeaway
Only ESVARBON-registered estate surveyors and valuers may practise, or hold themselves out as practising, estate surveying and valuation "for or in expectation of reward". Valuation opinions are treated as their exclusive preserve. The statutory penalties are nominal (old naira amounts), and a 2024 amendment bill to raise them has not been shown to have passed. Lagos law separately requires developers to price at values determined by an estate surveyor and valuer.

### Cited Findings
- Under s.16(1)(a) of the Estate Surveyors and Valuers (Registration, etc.) Act (Cap E13 LFN), it is an offence for an unregistered person "for or in expectation of reward" to practise "or hold himself out to practise" as an estate surveyor and valuer. Under s.16(1)(b), it is an offence to use any name, title or description implying registration — [PLAC Laws of Nigeria](https://www.lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=136)
- Penalties under s.16(4) — [PLAC](https://www.lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=136):
  - Lower court: a fine of up to ₦100, plus ₦20 per day for a continuing offence.
  - High Court: a fine of up to ₦1,000 or imprisonment of up to 2 years, plus ₦50 per day.
- The Act does not define "practice". The Board regulates "the practice of estate surveying and valuation" (s.2(d)). Section 17(1) bars unregistered persons from public appointments that require the status — [PLAC](https://www.lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=136)
- 2023 and 2024 House of Representatives bills proposed stricter penalties and penalties for unprofessional conduct. I found no evidence of passage — [NILDS](https://ir.nilds.gov.ng/handle/123456789/2564); [NILDS](https://ir.nilds.gov.ng/handle/123456789/2222)
- NIESV officials say giving opinions on value is the exclusive responsibility of ESVARBON-licensed estate surveyors and valuers — [NIESV Lagos](https://www.niesvlagos.org/en/esvarborn/); [NIESV](https://www.niesv.org.ng/esvarbon.php)
- ESVARBON chairman Dosu Fatokun pledged in March 2024 to enforce the Act — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/03/28/esvarbon-will-enforce-act-for-seamless-asset-valuation-says-fatokun/)
- At the 2025 Valuers' Assembly, ESVARBON flagged unqualified practitioners. It proposed "operation clear the holdouts" with law enforcement, and links with the EFCC and NFIU — [Vanguard, Nov 2025](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/11/esvarbon-celebrates-50-years-of-regulatory-excellence-at-2025-annual-valuers-assembly/)
- Estate surveyors and valuers are also listed DNFBP professions under the SCUML Regulations 2024 (reg. 4(3)(e)) — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- LASRERA Law s.32(3): a developer must charge consideration at fair market value as determined by an estate surveyor and valuer — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

### Inferences
- HomeAI should not issue or present "valuations", "fair price" or "market value" opinions as professional valuations unless an ESVARBON-registered surveyor signs them. AI price estimates should be labelled as non-professional indicative estimates.
- Physical audits and inspection jobs on the job board that are marketed as "valuation" or "survey" should be restricted to ESVARBON-registered members.
- Ordinary letting agency by non-surveyors is widespread and regulated in Lagos by LASRERA. The ESVARBON Act's application to fee-earning letting and management by non-surveyors is contested, and I found no case law confirming it either way.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative ruling or ESVARBON regulation listing which agency or management activities are reserved.
- I found no 2024–2026 prosecution data under s.16.

## 4. Lagos Tenancy Law 2011, the pending Tenancy and Recovery of Premises Bill (2025/2026), and the FCT position

### Takeaway
The Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 (Law No. 14) still governs as of the latest reports. It caps advance rent from new tenants at 1 year, with a ₦100,000 fine or 3 months' imprisonment. The Lagos State Tenancy and Recovery of Premises Bill 2025 would:
- cap agency commission at 5% of a year's rent;
- require LASRERA registration for agents;
- raise penalties to ₦1m.

The Bill passed second reading in July 2025 and was still at committee stage in May 2026 (reportedly still there in July 2026). It is NOT law as far as I could verify.

### Cited Findings
**Tenancy Law 2011**
- The 2011 Law (Law No. 14) has the following sections: "Advance Rent" s.4, service charges and deposits s.10, and "Length of Notice" s.13 — [Lagos State Gazette 2011 via PolicyVault](https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/NGA596.pdf)
- Under s.4, it is unlawful to demand or receive more than 1 year's rent from a new or prospective tenant. Sitting monthly tenants may not be charged more than 6 months in advance. The penalty is reported as a ₦100,000 fine or 3 months' imprisonment, or both. These details come from a blog, and sources differ on the sitting-tenant period — [Ownkey blog](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/tenant-rights-nigeria); [Ownkey](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/nigeria-real-estate-market-dynamics-advance-rent-supply-demand)
- Its present status: the Lagos 2011 Law still governed tenancies as of July 2026, according to a blog (less authoritative) — [mushrooms.ng](https://mushrooms.ng/blog/lagos-tenancy-bill-2026-explained)

**The pending Bill**
- The draft Bill is published by the Lagos House of Assembly — [Lagos House of Assembly](https://lagoshouseofassembly.gov.ng/home/lagos-state-tenancy-and-recovery-of-premises-bill-2025-draftlagos-state/)
- Key provisions of the 2025 draft Bill — [OAL review, 8 Sep 2025](https://oal.law/?p=1006020):
  - s.3(1): agents must be registered under the LASRERA Law.
  - s.3(4): commission capped at 5% of one year's rent.
  - s.3(5): dual collection, non-remittance and overcharging become offences, with fines of up to ₦1m or 2 years' imprisonment.
  - s.5: sitting tenants pay no more than 3 months in advance (monthly tenancy) or 1 year (yearly tenancy); new tenants no more than 1 year. The fine is ₦1m or 3 months (s.5(5)).
  - s.6(2): receipts required for every rent payment.
  - s.12: separate receipts for service charges, accounts every 6 months, and refund of deposits less damages.
  - s.14(1) default notice periods:

    | Tenancy type | Default notice |
    |---|---|
    | Tenant at will | 1 week |
    | Monthly | 1 month |
    | Quarterly or half-yearly | 3 months |
    | Yearly | 6 months |

  - s.14(2)–(4): the tenancy lapses on 2 or 3 months' arrears, with 7 days' notice.
  - s.32: virtual hearings allowed.
  - s.33: tenants can challenge "unreasonable" rent increases.
  - s.43: unlawful eviction carries up to ₦1m or 6 months' imprisonment.
  - s.2(2): employer housing, institutional housing and shelters are exempt.
  - The Bill would repeal the 2011 Law.
- Status: the Bill passed second reading in July 2025 and went before the House Committee on Housing. Agents would also have to remit rent within 7 working days and issue receipts. The article said the Bill "has not yet become law" — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/lagos-caps-commission-at-5-in-new-tenancy-bill-to-rein-in-estate-agents/)
- On 26–28 May 2026, Housing Commissioner Moruf Akinderu-Fatai said at the ministerial press briefing that the Bill was at committee stage. He confirmed the 5% commission cap, mandatory LASRERA registration, rent-increase justification and standard notice periods — [WithinNigeria, 30 May 2026](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/05/30/may-2026-how-lagos-plans-to-regulate-rent-hikes-under-new-tenancy-bill/); [Channels TV, 26 May 2026](https://www.channelstv.com/2026/05/26/lagos-unveils-new-tenancy-bill-to-regulate-rent-estate-agency-fees/); [Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/new-tenancy-law-for-lagosians-underway-says-lagos-government/)
- I found no report of a third reading or of assent by Governor Sanwo-Olu up to late October 2026 — [search of Telegraph/Legit/Freedom Online coverage](https://telegraph.ng/news/2026/05/27/lagos-unveils-tenancy-bill-to-regulate-rent-hikes-agency-fees/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/industry/1711907-key-issues-lagos-unveils-tenancy-bill-regulate-rent-hikes-agency-fees/)

**Earlier caps and legal fees**
- Earlier 5% agency fee caps in Lagos (2015 and 2022) were widely ignored. This comes from a blog — [Ownkey](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/real-estate-agent-commission-fees-nigeria-who-pays)
- The current statutory cap is 10% for lettings under the LASRERA Law s.32(2) — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- Legal fees: under the LASRERA Law, tenancy agreements must be prepared by a legal practitioner under the Legal Practitioners' scale of fees. The draft Tenancy Bill reviews did not mention a legal-fee cap — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf); [OAL](https://oal.law/?p=1006020)

### Inferences
- Build HomeAI's fee engine so the Lagos agency fee cap can be configured: 10% today, 5% if the Bill is enacted.
- Hard-block the advance rent field: no more than 12 months for new Lagos tenants, and 6 months for sitting monthly tenants under the 2011 Law. If the Bill passes, sitting monthly tenants would drop to 3 months.
- Default notice periods in lease templates should track the Bill's s.14 schedule, for readiness, but confirm against s.13 of the 2011 Law.
- Monitor the Lagos House of Assembly for third reading and assent.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the operative text of the 2011 Law. Its s.4 penalty and notice periods are not verified from the gazette.
- The 2011 Law is commonly said to exempt Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi and Victoria Island from some provisions. I did not verify this from a source.
- I found no FCT tenancy statute text.

## 5. Land Use Act 1978: C of O, Governor's consent, searches, and what a "verification" can legally mean

### Takeaway
All land is vested in the State Governor, or for the FCT in the President through the FCT Minister. A C of O is only prima facie, rebuttable evidence of title, not conclusive proof. Alienating a statutory right of occupancy requires Governor's consent. A platform cannot legally "certify" title; only the land registry or Governor's office and the courts determine it. HomeAI's tiers should be framed as document-presence and inspection checks, not title guarantees.

### Cited Findings
- Under s.1 of the Land Use Act 1978 (Cap 202 LFN 2004), land in each state is vested in the Governor for the benefit of Nigerians — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)
- A C of O is prima facie evidence of exclusive possession and a right of occupancy. Someone who proves a valid title predating the certificate can rebut it — *Ogunleye v Oni* (SC 193/1987, 1990) — [NigeriaLII](https://nigerialii.org/akn/ng/judgment/ngsc/1990/40/eng@1990-04-26)
- In *Adole v Gwar* (2008), the Supreme Court held that a C of O may "be effectively challenged and rendered invalid, null and void" — [NigeriaLII](https://nigerialii.org/akn/ng/judgment/ngsc/2008/7/eng@2008-04-04)
- Section 22 requires Governor's consent to alienate a statutory right of occupancy. *Savannah Bank v Ajilo* extended this to deemed rights. Case law differs on whether a non-consented transfer is void or voidable (*Osho v Foreign Finance Corp*) — [Cambridge JAL](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-law/article/is-the-grant-of-governors-consent-under-the-nigerian-land-use-act-automatic/E97EEC205CA03FA21107757453622443); [Omaplex](https://omaplex.com.ng/a-critical-examination-of-the-element-of-consent-in-mortgage-of-land-under-nigerian-law/)
- *Ibrahim v Obaje* (2017) held that consent does not apply to land not covered by statutory rights of occupancy, where the transfer is between private individuals — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2021/09/The-Recent-Decision-of-The-Supreme-Court-in-Yakubu-v.-Simon-Obaje-A-Coup-detat-Against-Governors-Consent-Under-the-Land-Use-Act.pdf)
- Good practice is a State Land Registry search plus confirmation of perfected Governor's Consent, to address multiple-sale and family-land fraud. Commercial verifiers such as Trustcrow and LandSafe rely on lawyers and registry officials to obtain official records — [Ownkey](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/nigeria-real-estate-buyer-due-diligence-guide); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/20/how-to-verify-land-or-property-in-nigeria-landsafe-is-the-answer/)
- Lagos increased land transaction fees, including Governor's Consent fees, stamp duties, registration fees, the Capital Contribution Levy and charting — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1104024)
- LASRERA Law s.32(1)(f): agents must not prepare legal documents for real estate transactions — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

### Inferences
- What HomeAI CAN state:
  - which documents were uploaded;
  - whether a registry search report from a licensed lawyer or surveyor was obtained, with its date and result;
  - utility-bill matches;
  - the date of the physical inspection and the inspector's identity.
- What HomeAI CANNOT state:
  - that a title is "valid", "genuine" or "free of encumbrance";
  - that consent is perfected, unless an official registry record or a lawyer's report supports it;
  - any valuation, unless it is an ESVARBON-signed report.
- Gold and Platinum tiers should require a registry search by a Nigerian legal practitioner or a registered surveyor, held on file.
- The liability risks are:
  - negligent misstatement;
  - FCCPC Act claims for misleading representations, depending on how tier labels are worded;
  - if HomeAI handles transactions, its own AML CDD failures.
- Recommended disclaimer elements:
  - a tier reflects only the listed checks, as of a stated date;
  - a tier is not a certification of title, a valuation or legal advice;
  - users must conduct an independent land registry search and engage a lawyer;
  - a C of O is not conclusive proof of title;
  - liability is limited to the extent the law permits.
- Disclaimers will not protect against misleading labels under consumer law, so tier names and copy should be accurate.

### Gaps
- I found no Nigerian case or regulation on a portal's liability for verification badges, and none on how courts enforce platform disclaimers.
- I did not verify the Lagos or FCT (AGIS) official search procedures and fees.

## 6. Short-let regulation and taxes (Lagos)

### Takeaway
I found no Lagos statewide short-let licensing or registration regime. Short-lets are caught by the Lagos Hotel Occupancy and Restaurant Consumption (HORC) Law 2009 5% consumption tax, collected by hosts as agents of LIRS. Its enforcement history has been contested in court. VAT and the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective January 2026) also apply. Private estates can and do ban short-lets: Banana Island did so in February 2026.

### Cited Findings
- The Lagos HORC Law 2009 is described as covering accommodation in hotels, guest houses, motels and short-let apartments. The tax is 5% of the bill, separate from VAT, with monthly collection and remittance. Operators act as collection agents with distraint powers against them. These come from search-result summaries of Mondaq commentary and a blog — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/article/1646430); [Ownkey short-let guide](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/how-to-manage-short-let-airbnb-nigeria); [LegalNaija](https://legalnaija.com/compliance-challenges-of-lagos-state/)
- Penalties for default are reported as up to 6 months in jail or a ₦500,000 fine. Collection has moved to electronic revenue assurance — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/life-arts/article/stiff-penalty-forces-hoteliers-compliance-e-revenue-collection/)
- Legal challenges:
  - The Supreme Court decided a constitutionality dispute over the Hotel Licensing and HORC laws in July 2013 — [PwC tax alert July 2013](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/pdf/tax-alert-july-2013.pdf)
  - In March 2018, the Federal High Court restrained enforcement of the 5% law and its 2017 regulations pending the case. I found no final outcome — [ThisDay, 24 Mar 2018](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2018/03/24/court-restrains-lagos-govt-from-enforcing-5-hotel-tax-law/)
- A LIRS registration form for the HORC law (2020) exists in the NGF repository — [NGF repository](https://ngfrepository.org.ng:8443/handle/123456789/3270)
- The Banana Island estate banned short-lets in February 2026 over security concerns. Similar restrictions may follow in Ikoyi, Victoria Island and Lekki Phase 1 — [BusinessDay, 19 Mar 2026](https://businessday.ng/companies/article/lagos-shortlet-market-growth-slows-to-n285-5bn-amid-ban-tax-changes/)
- The Lagos short-let market earned ₦281.03bn in 2025 and is projected at about ₦285.5bn for 2026. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025, effective January 2026, is cited as a market factor — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/companies/article/lagos-shortlet-market-growth-slows-to-n285-5bn-amid-ban-tax-changes/)
- VAT on short-let income is reported at 7.5%. The source is a blog, and older reports cite 5%, which is outdated — [Ownkey](https://ownkey.com/ng/blog/how-to-manage-short-let-airbnb-nigeria)
- "Hospitality industry" is itself a DNFBP category under the SCUML Regulations 2024 (reg. 4(2)(c)) — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

### Inferences
- HomeAI should not assume platform-level HORC collection duties. Hosts are the collection agents.
- HomeAI should still:
  - show the tax on bookings, or let hosts add it;
  - check estate bylaws and mark listings in estates that ban short-lets, such as Banana Island;
  - get legal advice on whether the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 creates any platform-level duty.
- Short-let hosts may themselves be DNFBPs under the "hospitality" category.

### Gaps
- I found no official Lagos Ministry of Tourism or LASRERA short-let registration scheme.
- I did not verify the current HORC enforcement status after 2018, or the exact Nigeria Tax Act 2025 provisions on short-let rentals and digital platforms.

## 7. AML/CFT: MLPPA 2022, TPPA 2022, SCUML and NFIU obligations

### Takeaway
Real estate dealers, developers, agents and brokers are DNFBPs under s.30 of the MLPPA 2022, supervised by SCUML (EFCC). New DNFBPs must register with SCUML before starting business. They must:
- run CDD and record identity, NIN and source of funds;
- identify both buyer and seller in sales;
- file STRs to the NFIU within 24 hours or "without delay", at any amount;
- file currency transaction reports over ₦5m (individuals) or ₦10m (companies) to SCUML within 7 days;
- file cash reports over US$1,000;
- file monthly nil returns;
- keep records for 5 years;
- screen against UN and Nigerian sanctions lists.

Penalties run to ₦250,000 per day (s.6), ₦1m per day (STR failures) and ₦10m–₦25m or prison for cash-limit breaches. SCUML also sets an administrative fines schedule.

### Cited Findings
**Statute and designation**
- The MLPPA 2022 was signed on 12 May 2022 and repealed the 2011 Act — [search summary of Mondaq/PwC](https://mondaq.co.uk/nigeria/money-laundering/1227796/a-synopsis-of-the-money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022<sup>1<sup>)
- Section 30 defines DNFBPs to include "(i) dealers in real estate, estate developers, estate agents and brokers" — [LawPadi MLPPA text](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- The DNFBP Regulations 2024 are S.I. No. 39 of 2024, made 15 May 2024 by the Attorney-General under s.28 MLPPA and s.95 TPPA, and published in Official Gazette No. 102 (17 June 2024). Reg. 4(2)(b) designates "dealers in real estate, estate developers, estate agents and brokers". Reg. 4(2)(i) designates mortgage brokers, reg. 4(2)(c) the hospitality industry, and reg. 4(3)(e) estate surveyors and valuers — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

**Registration**
- SCUML registers and certifies DNFBPs (s.17(2)(a) MLPPA) — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Existing businesses had to declare their activities to SCUML within 3 months of the Act's commencement (s.6) — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Reg. 5(3): "New businesses and professions shall, before the commencement of business, register with SCUML". DNFBPs in free trade zones must also register — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Reg. 6: licensing authorities and self-regulatory bodies must run AML and criminal background checks at entry. This could plausibly extend to LASRERA as a "Government Licensing Authority", which is my inference — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

**Cash limits**
- Section 2 MLPPA: no person shall, except through a financial institution, make or accept cash payments above ₦5,000,000 (individual) or ₦10,000,000 (body corporate) — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Section 19(2)(b) penalty for breach: ₦10m or at least 3 years' imprisonment, or both, for an individual; ₦25m for a body corporate — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Section 2(2): splitting transactions to evade reporting is an offence — [search summary of LawPadi/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/article/1218416)

**Customer due diligence**
- Section 6: identify the customer before any transaction above US$1,000 or its equivalent — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Reg. 29: standard CDD applies to all new business relationships, and to occasional cash transactions above $1,000 — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Reg. 40(4): "Real-estate agents, brokers and developers shall verify the identity of their clients including the purchasers and the vendors when involved in transactions for the sale or purchase of the property". Reg. 40(5) requires name, address, occupation, source of funds, account details and NIN. Reg. 40(7): linked transactions are aggregated toward thresholds — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Reg. 34: identify and verify beneficial owners of legal persons, and comply with the beneficial ownership rules in CAMA ss.119–123 — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Senior management approval is needed for PEP relationships. Monthly reports to SCUML are required on transactions for the public sector and for PEPs (reg. 38) — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

**Reporting**
- Suspicious transaction reports:
  - Section 7(2) MLPPA: file within 24 hours of the transaction. Penalty under s.7(10): ₦1,000,000 for each day the failure continues — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
  - Reg. 35: STRs go to the NFIU "without delay", which the Regulations define as immediately or within 72 hours. Attempted transactions are included, at any amount. Tipping-off is an offence, and good-faith reporters are protected. NFIU reporting formats must be used — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
  - In practice, the NFIU uses the goAML platform for filing — [Creodata](https://www.creodata.com/blog/nfiu-goaml-registration-nigeria/)
- Currency transaction reports:
  - Section 11 MLPPA: report within 7 days any single transaction, lodgment or transfer above ₦5m (individual) or ₦10m (body corporate). Penalty under s.11(3): ₦250,000 to ₦1,000,000 per day — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
  - Reg. 36: file CTRs above ₦5m or ₦10m to SCUML within 7 days, and file monthly nil reports. Foreign-currency domestic transfers above US$10,000 must be reported within 7 days. The Second Schedule says CTRs are filed weekly, with a ₦50,000 fine per unreported transaction per day of breach — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Cash-based transaction reports: reg. 37 requires a Standard Data Form for every cash transaction above US$1,000, sent to SCUML within 7 days, plus monthly nil returns — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- International transfers above US$10,000 must be reported to the NFIU, CBN and SEC within 1 day (s.3) — [search summary of LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)

**Record keeping and governance**
- Section 8 MLPPA and reg. 18: keep transaction and CDD records for at least 5 years after the transaction, or 5 years after an investigation closes. Records must include risk profile, name, address, NIN, currency type and amount, account numbers, ID documents, correspondence and analysis — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf); [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- Reg. 14: designate management-level Compliance Officers. Other duties include an AML/CFT/CPF policy, an internal audit, an annual training plan and quarterly training returns — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

**Terrorism and proliferation financing (TPPA 2022)**
- Reg. 19, made partly under s.95 TPPA: screen customers against the UN Consolidated List and the Nigeria Sanctions List. Apply targeted financial sanctions "without delay", and report frozen assets to the NFIU. DNFBPs must register for the Nigeria Sanctions Committee Alert System — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

**Penalties**
- Section 6(3) MLPPA: breach of DNFBP obligations carries a fine of ₦250,000 for each day the offence continues, plus suspension, revocation or withdrawal of licence by the licensing authority — [LawPadi](https://lawpadi.com/money-laundering-prevention-and-prohibition-act-2022/)
- SCUML administrative sanctions, Third Schedule (Professions / Businesses) — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf):

  | Breach | Professions | Businesses |
  |---|---|---|
  | Accepting cash above s.2 thresholds | ₦4m | ₦2m |
  | Failing to identify a customer with official ID before transacting | ₦2m | ₦1m |
  | Failing to declare activities to SCUML | ₦200k | ₦100k |
  | Unreported cash transaction over $1,000 | ₦50k each | ₦50k each |
  | No STR policy or controls | ₦2m | ₦1m |
  | Failing to file an STR | ₦2m | ₦1m |
  | Unreported ₦5m/₦10m transaction within 7 days | ₦100k each | ₦100k each |
  | Failing to screen sanctions lists | ₦1m | ₦500k |
  | Not registered on the Sanctions Alert System | ₦500k | ₦300k |
  | Operating without the requisite business licence | ₦2m | ₦2m |
  | Record-keeping failures | ₦300k + warning | ₦200k + warning |
  | Beneficial ownership failures | ₦500k | ₦250k |

  Criminal prosecution applies in aggravated cases.
- Reg. 39(5): paying a fine must be accompanied by evidence of registration and filings. SROs must revoke or suspend licences on SCUML's recommendation for persistent breach (reg. 39(6)) — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)
- Item 10 of the Third Schedule makes "operating a DNFBP without a requisite business or profession license" sanctionable. This creates an AML-level link to LASRERA permits — [SCUML Regulations 2024](https://scuml.efcc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EFCC-SCUML-Anti-Money-Regulation.pdf)

### Inferences
- If HomeAI earns commissions, brokers or holds buy-side escrow, it is very likely an "estate agent/broker" DNFBP. It should register with SCUML before launching those features, and register on goAML with the NFIU.
- HomeAI should build:
  - NIN-based KYC on landlords, tenants, buyers and sellers;
  - source-of-funds capture on sales;
  - a cash ban, or routing all payments via licensed financial institutions;
  - automated ₦5m/₦10m CTR and $1,000 cash-report triggers, with aggregation of linked payments;
  - monthly nil returns;
  - PEP and sanctions screening;
  - 5-year record retention;
  - a named Compliance Officer.
- Escrow should be held by a licensed bank or trustee. Holding client money directly raises separate CBN licensing questions, which are outside this scope.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve SCUML's current registration fee (believed to be free) or its online portal steps from scuml.org.
- I did not verify any SCUML sector-specific guideline for real estate issued after 2024.
- I found no 2025–2026 SCUML enforcement actions against real estate firms within budget.

## 8. Consumer complaints and the FCCPC

### Takeaway
The FCCPC (under the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018) is actively enforcing against real estate developers. It sealed Abuja and Lagos firms in 2026 over undelivered or unallocated properties and unpaid refunds. In Lagos, LASRERA's Committee of Inquiry and mediation are a parallel channel.

### Cited Findings
- In April 2026, the FCCPC sealed the offices of Paradise Estate (Life Camp, Abuja) over fully paid but undelivered units. The developer argued the dispute was contractual. The FCCPC said no court order restrained it — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fccpc-seals-paradise-estate-offices-over-buyers-unallocated-homes/); [NAN](https://nannews.ng/fccpc-seals-life-camp-paradise-estate-offices-over-property-squabbles/)
- On 21 May 2026, the FCCPC sealed Ochacho Group Worldwide and Ti'Bilon Construction and Real Estate in the FCT. The reasons were non-compliance with redress orders, undelivered properties and an unpaid ordered refund — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fccpc-seals-real-estate-firms-in-abuja-over-unresolved-customer-complaints/); [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/fccpc-seals-abuja-real-estate-firms-consumer-violations)
- In June 2026, the FCCPC sealed the Lagos office of PWAN MAX over 20 fully paid but unallocated plots, after an investigation that began in February 2025 — [Nairametrics, 8 Jun 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/08/fccpc-seals-pwan-max-office-over-alleged-regulatory-non-compliance/)
- The FCCPC has also moved against real estate operators for breaching compliance notices — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/?p=2844665)
- LASRERA takes public complaints and offers mediation, with MOUs enforceable as judgments — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2022/03/Review-of-the-Lagos-State-Real-Estate-Regulatory-Authority-Law-Newsletter.pdf)

### Inferences
- If HomeAI collects buy-side funds or brands properties "verified", the FCCPC can treat it as a service provider to consumers.
- HomeAI should therefore have:
  - a documented complaints process;
  - refund SLAs;
  - accurate verification marketing;
  - cooperation with redress orders.
- Seals of developers listed on HomeAI create reputational and escrow-release risk. HomeAI should monitor FCCPC actions against listed developers.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the FCCPC Act section numbers or penalty amounts, or any FCCPC rules on digital marketplaces.
- I found no aggregate statistics on FCCPC real estate complaints.
